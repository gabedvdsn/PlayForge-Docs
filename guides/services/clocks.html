Clocks

A clock is an IForgeClock, a source of time. PlayForge has one for Unity's time, one for the game's, an unscaled one, a scaled one and each entity's own, and a game can write its own.

Clock Its time
UnityClock Unity's Time, through UnityClock.Instance. It's the clock in force until code puts another in.
GameClock The game's, through GameClock.Instance: the clock in force, read each time it's asked. So it can be kept and handed on, and still follows a clock put in later. A sequence on it runs by the game's time, whatever its packet's owner's is.
UnscaledClock The game's without its time scale, through UnscaledClock.Instance. Its step is the clock in force's unscaled step, and its Time the unscaled time. So a sequence on it runs while the game's time is slowed or stopped, as a pause menu's does.
ScaledClock A scale of its own, ScaledClock.Scale, over another clock, its ScaledClock.Source (Scaled clocks).
EntityClock An entity's own, gas.GetClock(): the game's, at the entity's TimeScale (Entity clocks).

Each has what ForgeTime reads: DeltaTime, UnscaledDeltaTime, FixedDeltaTime, Time, UnscaledTime and FrameCount. Each also has FollowsTicks: whether a sequence on it waits for ProcessControl's ticks, where its tick rate is set.

FollowsTicks is true for a clock of the game's time. The ticks run on the game's time, and stand with it.

So UnscaledClock, which runs while that time stands, says false, and its sequences wait for Unity's frames instead. GameClock and a ScaledClock say as the clock they read does.

In this section

  • Scaled clocks: A ScaledClock runs at a scale of its own against another clock.
  • Entity clocks: An entity's clock, gas.GetClock(), runs at the entity's TimeScale: OwnScale times AttributeScale.
  • A clock of your own: A class of your own that implements IForgeClock is read as any clock is.
