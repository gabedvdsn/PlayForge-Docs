Clocks

A clock is an IForgeClock, a source of time: PlayForge has one for Unity's time, one for the game's, an unscaled one, a scaled one and each entity's own, and a game can write its own.

Clock Its time
UnityClock Unity's Time, through UnityClock.Instance: the clock in force until code puts another in
GameClock The game's, through GameClock.Instance: the clock in force, read each time it's asked, so it can be kept and handed on and still follows a clock put in later. A sequence on it runs by the game's time whatever its packet's owner's is
UnscaledClock The game's without its time scale, through UnscaledClock.Instance: its step is the clock in force's unscaled step and its Time the unscaled time, so a sequence on it runs while the game's time is slowed or stopped, as a pause menu's does
ScaledClock A scale of its own, ScaledClock.Scale, over another clock, its ScaledClock.Source (Scaled clocks)
EntityClock An entity's own, gas.GetClock(): the game's, at the entity's TimeScale (Entity clocks)

Each has what ForgeTime reads, DeltaTime, UnscaledDeltaTime, FixedDeltaTime, Time, UnscaledTime and FrameCount, and FollowsTicks: whether a sequence on it waits for ProcessControl's ticks where its tick rate is set. It's true for a clock of the game's time. The ticks run on the game's time and stand with it, so UnscaledClock, which runs while that time stands, says false, and its sequences wait for Unity's frames instead. GameClock and a ScaledClock say as the clock they read does.

Scaled clocks

new ScaledClock(scale, source) runs at its ScaledClock.Scale against its ScaledClock.Source: 1 as that one, 0.5 at half, 2 at double, 0 stopped; a scale below 0 is taken as 0. Its source is the clock it's given, or, with none, the clock in force. Its DeltaTime and FixedDeltaTime are its source's times its scale, and its unscaled times and its frame are its source's.

Its Time counts from the first time it's read, its source's time at each scale it had, so a change of its scale or its source carries on from where it was. Making one reads no clock, so a component's field can make one where it's declared. With no source, it counts whichever clock is in force as it's read: give it one to count by where a world of its own steps. The sequences that share one slow, speed up or stop together as it's set.

Entity clocks

An entity's clock, gas.GetClock(), runs at its TimeScale: OwnScale, the scale code sets, as a hit-stop's 0, times AttributeScale, the one its Time Scale attribute gives, read as each of the entity's steps begins, and 1 where its identity names none (Entities: Attributes). Setting TimeScale sets OwnScale, so a hit-stop holds under a slow, and the slow is still there after. Its DeltaTime and FixedDeltaTime are the game's times its scale, its Time its steps added up since it started, and its unscaled times and its frame the game's.

A scale of 0 holds the entity's time still: its effects, its cooldowns among them, and its abilities' waits and sequences stand. It isn't a pause, which ProcessControl's is (Processes: Pausing).

// A hit-stop on the entity struck: its own time held, its slows and hastes kept for after
var clock = gas.GetClock();
clock.OwnScale = 0f;
// ... a moment later
clock.OwnScale = 1f;

A clock of your own

A class that implements IForgeClock is read as any clock is: put in with ForgeTime.Use, named by a packet's Clock or a sequence's WithClock, or given by a clock pick of your own (Picking a clock in an inspector). Its FollowsTicks is true unless it says otherwise. GameClock, UnscaledClock, ScaledClock and EntityClock read the clock in force, so a world of its own's steps and the fixed tick drive them; a clock that reads Unity's Time itself doesn't follow them.
