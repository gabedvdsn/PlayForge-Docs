Fixed ticks

ProcessControl's fixed tick steps the game in fixed steps of its time, as many a frame as the frame's time holds, so a run comes out the same at any frame rate.

  • Fixed Tick Rate, under Fixed Tick in ProcessControl's inspector, or ProcessControl.FixedTickRate in code, is the ticks in a second of game time, as 60; 0, the default, steps once a frame. Max Ticks Per Frame, 8 by default, is the most a frame runs: a frame too long for them drops the rest of its time, so the game doesn't fall further behind.
  • In each tick, what waits for a tick goes on, then every process steps at Update and then at LateUpdate, its end of frame with it. While a tick runs, the clock in force is the tick's: ForgeTime.DeltaTime is its step, and ForgeTime.FrameCount the ticks so far. A process that steps at FixedUpdate steps with Unity's physics, as ever.
  • The ticks run on the game's time, so they stand while it does.

Code waits on them through ForgeTick:

ForgeTick Waits for
Next(token) The next tick, or, with ticks off, Unity's next frame, as a sequence's waits do
Next(timing, token) The same at a frame's timing: the Update family waits for a tick, and FixedUpdate, the physics step's, for Unity's
NextFrame(token) The next tick, or, with ticks off, Unity's next frame as UniTask.NextFrame waits for it
WaitUntil(condition, token) and WaitWhile(condition, token) A condition, asked once a tick, or once a frame with ticks off
Next(clock, token) The next step of a clock's time: Unity's next frame, where the scene ticks, for a clock whose FollowsTicks is false, as UnscaledClock's is

ForgeTick.Ticking says whether the tick in force ticks, and ForgeTick.Ticks how many it has run. ForgeTick.Scene is the scene's tick, a ForgeTickSource, which ProcessControl's rate turns on, and ForgeTick.Current is the tick in force: a world of its own's while it steps or one of its processes changes state, the scene's otherwise. A ProcessWorld its owner steps ticks once for each Step(deltaTime), so its sequences wait on its steps.
