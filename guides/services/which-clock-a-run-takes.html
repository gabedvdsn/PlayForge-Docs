Which clock a run takes

Everything PlayForge times goes by a clock, most by their owner's. An effect goes by the entity it's on, and a sequence's run by the first clock it finds.

  • An effect counts its time by the clock of the entity it's on, its duration and its period both. So a slowed entity's effects, its cooldowns among them, last longer. Its Delta Time Scaler changes it further (Effects: Duration).
  • An entity's own step goes by its clock: its input buffer's windows, its services, its turn and its barriers' decay.
  • A sequence's run, an ability's among them, takes the first clock it finds, in the order below.
  • A synced runner steps by its packet's clock, else its sequence's, else its packet's owner's (runner.ClockFor(data)).
  • A cue's packet carries its owner's clock: the activation's for an ability's cue, else that of the one it plays on. A Sequence cue's run goes by its Clock pick, else the sequence's own, else that one (Cues: Data packets).
  • Code of your own reads its owner's step with ForgeTime.DeltaTimeFor(owner), as ProjectileMonoProcess moves by its handler's. A task reads its run's with SequenceTaskLibrary.DeltaTime(token), 0 while the run is paused. TaskSequenceRuntime.ClockOfRun(token) is the run's clock.

Anything else goes by the game's time, the clock in force.

The clock a sequence's run takes

A sequence's run, an ability's among them, takes the first clock it finds, in this order:

  1. its own, runtime.Clock, which what runs it sets: a TaskSequenceProcess's Clock, a chain's Clock or an ability's Run Sequence task's pick;
  2. its packet's, data.Clock;
  3. its sequence's, WithClock on the builder;
  4. its packet's owner's: an ability's caster's own clock;
  5. else the game's, GameClock.Instance.

Its waits, delays, timeouts, longest runs and ElapsedTime go by it, and so do its tasks' tweens and its synced tasks (Task Sequences: Clocks).

An ability runs on its activation's packet. So its waits go by its caster's clock, unless the packet names another.
