Process and sequence logs

ProcessControl and task sequences have switches of their own that decide whether they say what they do, and most of what they say is information, which the level must let through to show.

ProcessControl, of the Processes system (Processes: Logging):

  • Output Logs, off by default, logs each process registered and unregistered, the scene's processes it collects as it starts and the hierarchy it makes of them, and, as a warning, a change of state its rules don't cover.
  • Detailed Logs, on by default, adds to those while Output Logs is on: each process's handler, each process collected, and each process's state as it's queued, set and forced.
  • A world of its own has the same switches, ProcessWorld.OutputLogs and ProcessWorld.DetailedLogs; a scene's world's are its ProcessControl's.

Sequences, of the Sequences system (Task Sequences: Logging):

  • A run that fails is logged as an error, with what it threw, unless its sequence's WithErrorLogging(false) says otherwise; a stage whose OnException hears a throw and lets it on logs it too, unless the stage's WithErrorLogging(false) says otherwise. A handler that throws, as an OnComplete, is logged as an error.
  • WithInjectionLogging(true), on a sequence or a stage, logs each injection as it's applied: its kind, where it came from and the stage.
  • A chain's WithLogging(true) logs its progress: each sequence it starts, finishes or skips, the conditions that fire, its repeats and its interruptions.

Debug tools say what they find as information too: TagCache.LogWeights(), of Tags; the Threshold Debugger effect worker, ThresholdDebuggerEffectWorker, of Workers; and a queued LogAction, of General.
