PlayForge — The Creator Lab

A space for trying a project's own assets: put units down, give them abilities, effects and items, cast, and watch the numbers move. Open it from Window → PlayForge → Creator Lab: it opens in a new scene and plays. The scenes open before are saved first, if you want them saved.

The lab plays every PlayForge asset in the project's own folders.

The space

  • The ground is a plane in the middle of the scene, lit from above.
  • The camera looks down at an angle. Scroll to pull it back or bring it in; drag with the middle button to pan. The right panel's sliders set its distance and tilt, and Reset view puts it back.
  • Units are a body and a GameplayAbilitySystem: a capsule with a nose to show where it faces, a ring at its feet in its side's color, and a bar over it with its name and health. A unit walks in a straight line where it's sent and attacks what it's set on, and a walk or a stop ends a turn to face its abilities ordered, as a game's move would. PlayForge has no movement of its own, so the lab's is its own: a project's game moves its units its own way.
  • The lab's caster is a unit of the lab's own, on no side, with no body. It's the source of what you drop while no unit is selected.

The panels

Each panel collapses to a tab at its edge with its − button.

  • Library (left): the active Forge Project's assets by kind, abilities, effects, items, entities, attribute sets, attributes and scalers, each kind by name, or every asset of the project while it has no Forge Project. Project switches the active project, as The Forge does, and follows The Forge's switch. The search box filters them; Refresh lists them again after you make or delete one. A kind's color shows in its icon alone.
  • Selected (middle): hidden until something is selected. Click an asset in the library to see its numbers level by level, as "−80/−120/−160 Health"; Open in The Forge opens it there. Click a unit to see its attributes, tags, effects with their stacks and time left, abilities with their levels and cooldowns, and items. There, − and + change an ability's level, × takes an ability or an item, and Remove takes the unit out.
  • Lab (right): the mode, the test dummies, the camera and the lab's time.
  • Log (bottom): collapsed until you open it, a page for each way the lab shows its working; see below.

Dropping assets

Drag an asset from the library and drop it on the space:

Dropped On What happens
An effect A unit It lands on the unit, from the selected unit, or from the lab's caster
An ability A unit, or the ground The selected unit, or the lab's caster, casts it there, its target in the activation's data, as click targeting takes it with no click. A unit that doesn't know it learns it first. An ability that needs more than the point waits for a click, which Esc cancels
An item A unit It's given to the unit, equipped
An entity or an attribute set The ground A test dummy of it is put down, on the side the Lab panel's picker names

Test dummies

The Test dummies section puts down a dummy of any entity or attribute set, on a side: Blue, Red, Green, Gold or Neutral. A dummy counts what it takes: its damage a second, the last blow by its impact types and the effect it came from, and a short history, which the Selected panel shows. It doesn't die unless asked: falling, it's revived where it stands, at full. Left alone for three seconds, it mends to full. Reset puts each dummy back where it was put, at full; Clear takes every unit out.

An effect or an entity changed in the inspector or The Forge as the lab plays is read on its next application, as every asset is. What a unit was made from, its attribute set and its starting abilities, shows on a new unit.

Battle

The Battle mode fights teams of bots. Pick an entity and a side above, a level in Level, and Add bot; each side stands in a line of its own, Blue to the south, Red to the north, Green to the west and Gold to the east. Quick battle makes Blue against Red, A side of each, of the entity selected in the library, or picked at random from the project's. Play starts the fight and the lab's time; the line under the buttons says who stands, and who won, or that no one stands. Reset makes every bot anew where it started, at full, with the items and abilities you gave it since and their levels.

A bot takes the nearest foe it can target, casts what's ready, and otherwise attacks with its attack. Its order and its moments are its rules':

  • Cast Order Key: the Local Data key an entity's order is under, as "W Q E", the letters its abilities other than its attack, in its entity's order. With none, each in turn.
  • Cast When Key: the key for when it casts an ability: InRange (the default), Hurt (below 60% health), Low (below 35%, away from its foe, as an escape), Stunned (while something stops it) or FoeLow (its foe below half).
  • Reach Key: the key for an ability's reach, 0 for anywhere, where it has no reach of its own, as a Cast Range; one that has reaches as far as that does.

Low on health, a bot backs off a moment once in a while. The Demo's AI hero is a bot, its roster giving the keys.

Your unit

In either mode you can play a unit of your own. Put down in the Lab panel's Your unit section puts one of the entity picked above down on the side picked, where the camera looks, and gives you control of it; Take control takes the unit selected, and a bot taken over stops fighting until you let it go, a new fight passing it over and Reset keeping you on its seat's new bot. Release lets it go, and Follow has the camera follow it. The line under the buttons says whom you control.

The controls are the Demo's: right-click the ground to walk there, a foe to attack it, or an ally to walk to it; Q, W, E and R cast its abilities past its attack, in its entity's order, and 1 to 6 its items' active abilities; an ability that needs a target waits for a left-click, and Esc cancels it; S stops it. They read PlayForge's input, ForgeInput, so a game's own input or a test's stands in.

Its orders go through a driver. The lab's walks a unit in a straight line and casts as the lab's units do. A project whose units move and cast their own way makes a Lab Driver asset (Create → PlayForge → Lab Driver) of its own class, overriding the orders its game gives its own way (MoveTo, Attack, Cast, Stop, each doing as the lab's does unless overridden), and names it on its Lab Rules' Driver.

Time

The lab's units live in a world of the lab's own (ProcessWorld), which the lab steps by its own time, so its time stands still or runs faster without touching Unity's. Pause stops it, Step goes one frame (a sixtieth of a second) while it's paused, and Speed runs it from a quarter to four times as fast. The camera and the panels go on while it's paused.

Showing its working

The bottom panel's pages show what happens as the lab plays. Each records only while the lab runs.

  • Journal: the lab's effect journal over every unit: each effect landing, with its number traced, refused and why, ticking, hitting, coming off, and each worker that ran, by its hook and its side, with the actions it queued and whether they ran; newest first, on the selected unit or, with Selected unit off, on any. Click an entry to see its parts: a number's, a hit's breakdown, or a worker's actions.
  • Combat: the selected unit's combat log, newest first, each hit with its steps under it, "-30 as made → Math -30 → -15 (inline) → landed -15". The project's combat log writes it (Project Settings → PlayForge → Combat Log).
  • Stops: a list of what pauses the lab's world: an effect lands, is refused or has a worker run, an attribute crosses a value, a tag comes or goes, an ability is refused, each on the selected unit or any, and naming an effect, an ability, a tag or an attribute where it asks for one (Any for an effect or an ability takes any). As one fires, the world pauses and the page says which and what fired it. Go plays on, Step Frame goes one frame, and Step Tick goes on until an effect ticks on the selected unit, or on any, up to ten seconds, or a stop fires.
  • Watches: pin a number on the selected unit, an attribute's current or base value, a tag's count, an effect's stacks or time left, or a Scaler asset read on it, and see each as a line over the last minute of the lab's time, in its own color and scaled to its own range, with its value now.
  • Log: PlayForge's messages as ForgeLog lets them through, by system, newest first, those about the selected unit where Selected unit is on. System and Level give a system a level of its own, which the Console keeps too; As all gives it back to the Logging preference's.

The lab's rules

The lab needs to know a few things about a project: which attribute is a unit's health, which it walks at, which tags stop, hide or shield it, and which ability is its attack. With no rules given, it takes the attributes playing the project's Health and Movement Speed roles (ATTRIBUTES.md), or else finds the attributes named Health and Movement Speed (or Move Speed), and walks a unit without a speed at 4 a second. To say more, make a Lab Rules asset (Create → PlayForge → Lab Rules) and give it to the lab's CreatorLab component:

  • Health and Movement Speed, or Walk Speed where a unit has none. Left empty, each is the project's.
  • Stops: tags that stop a unit moving, as Stunned. Hides: tags under which the other side can't see or target it, as Invisible. Shields: tags under which no one can target it.
  • Attack Range Key: the Local Data key a unit's attack range is under, or Attack Range for every unit; a unit whose attack has a reach of its own, as a Cast Range, attacks from as far as it reaches.
  • Attack Slot: which of an entity's starting abilities is its attack, from 0, or −1 for none.
  • Driver: the project's Lab Driver, which the unit you control is ordered about through (Your unit).

The Demo's roster is the Demo's rules: its units, camera and dummy are the lab's, so the two can't drift.

In code

CreatorLab.Create(library, rules, panels) makes a lab, as the tests do. Its calls are the panels': AddUnit and AddDummy, ApplyEffect, CastAt, GiveItem, Drop (at a point of the screen) and DropAt (on a unit or a point), Select, SetMode, ResetDummies, Clear, and Step, Paused, Speed and StepFrame for its time. A library is an ILabLibrary: in the editor every asset of the project (EditorLabLibrary), in code a ListLibrary of the assets given; a game's own build gets ListLibrary.Of(project), the assets its Forge Project's catalog names, which the lab plays where no editor gives it a library. A library offers projects to switch between through ILabLibrary.Projects, Project and UseProject, which have defaults. The unit you control is ControlledUnit, given by PutDownOwnUnit, TakeControl and ReleaseControl, its camera by Follow; the lab's controls are its Controls, a LabControls, whose Driver comes before its rules'. A game's own player can be a LabControls too, as the Demo's is: its unit set on it, or its ControlledUnit() overridden.

What it shows of its working is its Showing, a LabShowing: its Journal, an EffectJournal over every unit; its Stops, added with Add(stop), and StoppedBy and StoppedFor as one fires, Resume() to let it go and StepTick(); its Watches, pinned with Pin(watch); and its Log of ForgeLog's messages.

A build can leave the lab out by defining PLAYFORGE_NO_LAB. The Demo uses the lab's units, so it goes too.
