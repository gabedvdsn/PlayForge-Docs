Task Sequences

A task sequence is a workflow over time, built in code: stages run one after another, each a set of tasks run together. An ability runs as one, and so can anything with steps: a projectile's flight, a channel, a cutscene, a tutorial, a boss's phases. A sequence runs as a process (Processes), so ProcessControl starts, pauses and ends it like any other.

The Task Sequence Examples sample shows every part of this page in code, and a component that runs any example from a scene.

In this guide

  • The parts: Sequence: its stages, in order, with what holds for all of them: its name, its conditions, a timeout or a longest run, whether it repeats, and the handlers that hear it end.
  • Building one: Build gives the definition, which a TaskSequence runs and a synced runner is built from; BuildSequence gives the sequence itself, and BuildSyncRunner a synced runner (below).
  • Waiting: Delays: Delay(seconds) is a stage of its own that waits; DelayUntil and DelayWhile wait on a predicate, and WaitUntil is a stage that does.
  • Repeating: WithRepeat(true) on the builder repeats the whole sequence until it's interrupted.
  • Changing the run: From a task, through its data packet
  • Time limits and endings: WithTimeout(seconds, injection) injects when the time is up, a skip by default; WithMaxDuration(seconds) ends the run, or the stage, at its longest.
  • Clocks: A run's time is its clock's: its waits and delays, its timeouts and longest runs, its ElapsedTime, the library's tweens and its synced tasks all go by it.
  • Running one: RegisterAndRun(data), or ProcessControl.Register(sequence).With(data).Run(), runs it as a process: it shows in the Debugger, pauses with ProcessControl, and ends with its scene.
  • Chains: A chain runs its sequences in order with one data packet; it can repeat, pause and resume, skip its current sequence, or be interrupted, and takes conditions of its own (InterruptWhen, SkipSequenceWhen).
  • Synced sequences: A synced runner is the same sequence stepped a frame at a time, with no async: a process with the Synchronous lifecycle calls its Step each frame.
  • The libraries: SequenceTaskLibrary has tasks to await inside a stage's Task, each taking the task's token, and SyncSequenceTaskLibrary the same tasks as ISequenceTasks for a synced runner, or for any stage
  • Logging: What a sequence says goes through ForgeLog, under its own system, Sequences: a handler's throw as an error, and, where a sequence's or a chain's logging is on (WithInjectionLogging, a chain's WithLogging)…
  • Built-in kinds: The kinds PlayForge ships for this guide's pickers, each with its menu and what it does, as its picker says.
