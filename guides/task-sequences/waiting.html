Waiting

  • Delays: Delay(seconds) is a stage of its own that waits; DelayUntil and DelayWhile wait on a predicate, and WaitUntil is a stage that does. SyncDelay waits inside a stage, as one of its tasks.
  • The library's waits, inside a task: SequenceTaskLibrary.Delay, WaitFrames, Yield, WaitUntil and WaitWhile.
  • The player's input, read through ForgeInput, so a game's own input or a test's stands in: WaitForKey, WaitForAnyKey, which returns the key pressed, and WaitForPointer.

Each of the library's waits holds while its sequence is paused, and a press while it's paused isn't heard. A task that keeps time itself should read SequenceTaskLibrary.DeltaTime(token), a step of its run's clock (below), which is 0 while the sequence is paused.

A task that links its token, to cancel a wait of its own without its stage, links it through TaskSequenceRuntime.CreateLinkedTokenSource(token), whose token belongs to the task's run, so a wait on it keeps the run's clock and holds while the run is paused. A token that CancellationTokenSource.CreateLinkedTokenSource links belongs to no run, so its waits go by the game's clock and don't hold. Dispose the source once done, as any:

.Task(async (data, token) =>
{
    // Cancelled with the stage, or on its own through charge.Cancel(), and by the run's clock either way
    using var charge = TaskSequenceRuntime.CreateLinkedTokenSource(token);
    await SequenceTaskLibrary.Delay(1.5f, charge.Token);
})
