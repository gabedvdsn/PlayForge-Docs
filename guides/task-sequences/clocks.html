Clocks

A run's time is its clock's. These all go by it:

  • its waits and delays;
  • its timeouts and longest runs;
  • its ElapsedTime;
  • the library's tweens;
  • its synced tasks.

A run takes the first clock it finds:

  1. Its own, runtime.Clock, which whatever runs it sets: a chain's Clock, a TaskSequenceProcess's, or an ability's Run Sequence task's pick. So a packet others share keeps its own.
  2. Its packet's, data.Clock, which whatever starts the run sets.
  3. Its sequence's own, WithClock(clock) on the builder.
  4. Its packet's owner's: an ability's caster's own clock, its time scale times the game's.
  5. The game's, GameClock.Instance, which reads the clock in force, ForgeTime.Clock, as it's asked.

In this section

  • Clocks you can use: Besides the game's clock, a run can go by an unscaled clock, a scaled clock, an entity's clock or a clock of your own.
  • Reading a run's clock: Code reads a run's clock from its runtime, from a task's token, or from a synced runner.
  • Picking a clock in an inspector: An inspector picks a clock with an AbstractClockChoice field, marked [SerializeReference].
  • Clocks and fixed ticks: Under ProcessControl's fixed tick, a sequence waits for the next tick.
