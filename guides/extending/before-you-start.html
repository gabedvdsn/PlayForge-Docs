Before you start

This section covers what every kind of a game's own shares: the assembly it lives in, how the editor finds it, how its picker names and files it, how its fields show, where it keeps what it counts, and how it's renamed.

In this section

  • The assembly: A kind of a game's own compiles in an assembly that references PlayForge's.
  • How a kind is found: The editor finds a kind of a game's own by its base, so nothing registers a kind: writing the class is enough.
  • How a kind is named and filed: [ForgeMenu] on a kind's class says where its picker files it and what it's called.
  • How its fields show: A kind's fields draw under its picker as Unity draws their types.
  • One object for every holder: A kind is one object on its asset, shared by every entity the asset reaches.
  • Renaming a kind: A kind is saved by its class's name, assembly and namespace, so renaming one needs care.
