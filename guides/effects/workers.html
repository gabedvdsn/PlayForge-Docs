Workers

Effect workers react to this effect's own events. A worker can act when the effect is applied, when it ticks, when it is removed, and when it deals a change to an attribute (Workers maps each, and on whose side it runs). Workers return actions, which run at the end of the frame. An Instant effect runs its apply and remove workers within its one application.

A worker can also answer numbers: a magnitude of the Worker kind asks the effect's workers for a value under a key, and one that implements INumberWorker answers, as the Attribute Change worker answers how much health the target has lost since the effect began. What a worker keeps for one application of its effect goes in ctx.GetState<T>(this), since the worker itself is shared by every application (Scalers: Asking a worker).

Worker Group, after them, gives the one the effect is on more workers while it lasts, as an item's group does while it's equipped: attribute workers, as a debuff that changes how its target takes damage; impact, tag and analysis workers, as Ice Blast's debuff shattering its target as its Health falls below 13% by a Threshold. They come back as it goes. Two of the effect on one target give the group once, and the last of them to go takes it back. An Instant effect doesn't last, so its group does nothing.

On Hit

On Hit, above the workers, holds the effects the hits of the one this effect is on apply while it lasts: a poisoned blade, a boon's Weak. Its entries are an item's: an attribute, the effects, to the one hit or the holder, only on a kill, impact types and a condition (Items: On Hit). An Instant effect doesn't last, so its On Hit does nothing. Another effect that replaces it under its tag takes it off, and a load puts it back once.

Proc Coefficient, in Impact, is what the effect's own hits carry for On Hit effects: theirs scale their chance and their duration by it, so a damage over time at 0.25 sets them off a quarter as often, for a quarter as long, and 0 sets nothing off. With Own Proc Coefficient off, its ability's stands, or else 1, and Proc Coefficient is hidden.

An effect has no lifesteal of its own: what its hits heal its caster is the caster's Lifesteal, the attribute the Lifesteal worker names as its Share, which the worker heals by, the one the project gives every entity or the caster's own (Workers: Lifesteal). For hits that steal more, an effect raises its caster's Lifesteal while they should: a lasting effect of +0.3 Lifesteal whose Removal condition takes it off, as a charged attack's buff comes off as its last charge is spent. The heal is a gain like any other, so Healing Received scales it, and damage a return sends back heals nothing.

Resolve Order

The project's Resolve Order, under Forge Project in Project Settings → PlayForge, decides the order an area's effects land on those it takes, so specific behaviour can be coded, as a totem whose aura keeps its allies alive resolving after them. Each entry is a Tag and an Order: one carrying the tag, or one under it, resolves at its Order, lower first, and one carrying none of the entries' tags at 0. One carrying several takes the first entry it matches, so the list's order is its precedence. An entity carries a tag on its identity, as a totem always does, or while something grants it, as an aura can grant its holder one while it holds. The area's own order breaks ties.

Apply Effects and Apply/Remove Effects land on an ability's targets in it, though the targeting keeps its own order and its primary target; an effect's Area component lands on its target and those it takes in it, its target among them; and an aura puts its effect on those that came near in it. The tasks' and the Area's Use Resolve Order, on by default, turns it off for that one. Code reads it through ForgeResolveOrder: OrderOf(target), Sort(targets), and TargetsOf(data) for an activation's targets in order.

A death waits for its entity's end of frame, and an aura comes off a dead holder at its next check or as the holder ends, so one area's hits resolve together: a blow that kills the totem doesn't take its aura off the hero the same explosion strikes next. The order matters for what changes as each lands, as an effect that lands on the caster with each hit or a transfer.

Modifiers

Modifiers, under On Hit, change other effects as they're made, for that application alone: a boon that adds a burst to every Chill its holder applies, a ring that makes its holder's debuffs last longer, a cloak that halves the stuns landing on its wearer. An effect's hold on the one it's on while it lasts; an item's, an ability's and an entity's modifiers are the same entries. Each entry is a rule of its own, or names a shared Effect Modifier asset (Create → PlayForge → Effect Modifier), whose rule it uses in its place.

A rule has:

  • Direction: Outgoing, its holder's own effects as it applies them, on another or itself; Incoming, the effects landing on its holder, whoever applies them; or Both.
  • Effect Tags: the effects it changes, each with one of these among its asset tag and context tags, or a child of one, so Status.Chill picks every Chill. None picks every effect.
  • When: a condition read as the effect is made, its caster the caster and its target the target.
  • Changes, each of a kind:
    • Add Contained Effect: an effect that lands as this one lands, each tick, or as it ends, on the target or, with Lands On set to Caster, on its caster.
    • Change Impact: a Value Pipeline stage its impacts on an attribute go through, of some impact types where it names any. An impact that commits, a hit or a tick, runs it after its own pipeline, and its breakdown keeps it; a lasting impact, which no pipeline runs on, takes it on its magnitude, as Weak's −30% made −40%.
    • Add Overflow Effect: an effect that lands as an application would take it past its most stacks, on the target or its caster as its Lands On says, and Clear Stacks, which takes its stacks off with it. It needs a stack bound of the effect's own to overflow.
    • Change Duration: its time multiplied, then more added; its ticks spread over the time it has.
    • Change Stacks: more it can hold at most, where a stack bound of its own sets a most, and more a landing gives.
    • Change Chance to Apply: multiplied, then added to.
    • Grant Tags: more tags it grants while it's on.

An effect a rule adds, and what that effect contains, isn't changed by that rule again, so a Chill that adds a Chill adds one. Two of an item modify twice.
