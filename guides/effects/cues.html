Cues

What the effect shows and sounds, apart from what it changes, plays from its Cues component, from the Components list's +, a list for each of its moments: On Apply as it lands; On Reapply as an application it takes in lands, as a stack or a refresh; On Tick as each tick executes; On Hit as an impact it deals lands, on the one it lands on, its magnitude the value it changed, as for a damage number; On Remove as a lasting effect comes off; While Active while it lasts, from as it lands, or a load puts it back, to as it comes off, ending as it began where another application replaced it; On Refused as an application is refused, as by an immunity, on the one it was aimed at; and On Stack Change as its stacks change while it lasts, its magnitude the stacks it has. An effect holds one at most. A cue plays at each moment whose list holds it: a sound on each tick is a Sound in On Tick, and one as the effect lands as well is in On Apply too. An applier can say where an application hit, spec.WithHit(point, normal, magnitude), and its cues play there (Cues: Raises and sinks).

Each list's cues are listed as its workers are, each cue's kind picked from the list:

  • Camera Shake: shakes the camera through the shaker the game gives ForgeCues.Shaker, an ICameraShaker. With none given, it shakes nothing. Its Strength and Duration, as Prefab's Lifetime and Sound's Volume, are number fields read for its effect as it plays.
  • Sound: plays its clip where its Placement says, at the target by default (Cues: Placement).
  • Prefab: places its prefab where its Placement says, at the target by default, for Lifetime seconds, or, in While Active, for as long as the effect lasts, following the target. A cue raised at a point, as a Play Cue task's, places it there. With Pooled it takes its objects from a pool and gives them back, ForgeCuePool, the pool making Preallocate of them ahead as it first plays, and an IPooledCue on the prefab resets it as it's taken and given back.
  • Floating Text: floats a text where its Placement says, as a hit's damage or a status's name (Cues: Floating text).
  • Process: runs a MonoBehaviour process where its Placement says, on a data packet loaded for the cue, as a shield's while it lasts (Cues: Data packets).
  • Sequence: runs a task sequence on a data packet loaded for the cue, as a flicker while the effect lasts (Cues: Handlers).
  • Cue Tag: plays what its tag routes to (Cues: Routes).

A save's load ends each While Active cue and begins it again as the effect leaves and comes back, and plays no burst. Write a cue of your own by deriving from AbstractCueHandler and overriding the moments it plays at, or Burst to play every burst one way, reading the moment that raised it from cue.Moment.

Every cue plays through ForgeCues, an effect's as any other's. A game's own code raises one there too, as sparks where a ray hit: ForgeCues.Burst(sparks, new CueContext(entity, ECueMoment.Raised, point: hit.point)). A cue's context says what raised it, who's behind it, where it plays and, for a lasting cue, the key its beginning and its end share. ForgeCues.Enabled = false silences every cue, as on a server or while a simulation runs ahead, and ForgeCues.Mute(entity, true) one entity's, as one off screen; a lasting cue that began still ends. ForgeCues.Played hears each cue as it plays, for a debugger or a recorder.

A Cue Tag cue plays what its tag routes to, in place of naming what it shows, so an effect, an ability and an entity can each raise Cue.Hit.Fire and one place says how it looks. The project's Cue Library, an asset its settings name, plays the route for the tag, or else for its nearest parent, and a route that doesn't Override plays its parent's after it. An entity's Cue Receiver component plays a tag its own way first, in place of the library's, or before it with Pass Through; its Variant plays the library's route under its name, as Cue.Hit.Golem for Cue.Hit, where the library has one. Cues brings cues together: where each is raised, the library's routes, what each tag plays, and pooling.
