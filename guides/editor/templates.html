Templates

An asset can take another of its type as its template, as a prefab instance takes its prefab: it's the template's child, takes the template's fields when the template is pushed, and keeps the fields it overrides. This section covers setting a template, pushing it, overrides and what a push leaves. It's apart from the project's templates that Create copies (New assets and presets).

Setting a template

Set Template..., in the asset menu of an inspector's header, picks the template from a searchable list of the active project's other assets of the type. An asset that takes this one as its template, itself or through others, isn't listed, so no loop can be made. Unassign Template "Name" takes the template away. Either is one Undo step, and starts the asset with no overrides. The line under the asset's name in its header names its template.

Pushing

Push to Its Children, in the template's asset menu, read as Push to Its Child or Push to Its 3 Children, gives each child the template's fields, and each of their children theirs, as one Undo step. Saving a template changes no child: only a push does.

  • A plain field takes the template's value, unless the child overrides it.
  • A [SerializeReference] field, as an ability task or a worker, is copied whole, a new object of its own, unless the child overrides it.
  • A list is merged: the elements the template added since the last push are added, those it removed since are removed, and those the child added itself stay.
  • A push leaves each asset's identity, its name, description, textures and asset tag, and its own template. It leaves an ability's, effect's, item's or entity's level link too.

Overrides

An edit to a child's field, in its inspector, through Import or through a script's SerializedObject, makes the field the child's own, an override. Its inspector marks it with a bar at its left and its label in bold, as Unity marks a prefab instance's override. An edit inside a [SerializeReference] object overrides the whole object; an edit inside a list overrides nothing, as lists merge; and an edit to a field a push leaves, as the name, overrides nothing. Undo of the edit takes its mark back too.

A marked field's right-click menu has Revert to Template "Name", which takes the template's value and makes the field the template's again, as one Undo step.

In code

ForgeTemplatePush does what the menus do: ChildrenOf(template), PushToChildren(template), which gives back the assets it changed, and RevertToTemplate(property). ForgeTemplatePropagator is the push's engine, which records no Undo of its own: PropagateToChild(template, child), PropagateToAllChildren(template), FindChildAssets(template), ChildrenByTemplate(type), IsUsedAsTemplate(asset), WouldPropagationModifyAny(template, children), UpdateOverrides(child), which marks overridden the fields that differ from the template and clears the rest, and CaptureInitialListSnapshots(child), which records the template's lists as the child's starting point, so a push adds only what the template adds after it. ForgeTemplateConfig says which fields a push leaves: IsExcluded(path, type), GetAllExclusions(type), and SupportsTemplates(type), which is true for an ability, effect, item, entity or attribute set. The asset's side is BaseForgeAsset's: Template, HasTemplate, SetTemplate, ClearTemplate and its overrides.
