PlayForge — Scalers

A scaler is a value source that a magnitude field reads at a level, such as a table of damage per level. The level is the origin's, such as the level of the ability that applies an effect. Each section below matches what the inspector shows.

A magnitude field

A magnitude field, such as an effect's Magnitude or Duration, is a number field (ScalableFloat, or ScalableInt for a whole number). It shows its name, then its kind, then what it reads, then its field menu, which opens as wide as its items. The kind menu lists Constant, Table, By Level, Shared, × Attribute and Set By Caller at its top, then the kinds that read the game (below): Asset Part, Group, Group Impact, Local Data, Relative to a Field and Worker. It files the rest by category: the scalers of every kind under Scaler, Effect Stacks and Tracked Impact under Effect, Item Stacks under Item, and a kind of a project's own under the category it names. A field whose code declares what its context provides, with [NumberContext], offers only the kinds that can be worked out there: a targeting task's range, which has no target yet, offers no kind that reads one.

Every number an asset authors is a number field, read at the level of what holds it: an ability's, as its Buffer Window and its tasks', at its level; an effect's, as its Stack Bounds' Min and Max, its Starting Stacks and a Stacks change's, at the effect's; a cue's Strength, Duration, Lifetime and Volume, for its effect as it lands; an item's Max Stack Size at its own level, and a set bonus's Pieces at its holder's; and an entity's own, as a barrier's Decay Per Second, an attribute set's Snap Interval, an aura's Interval and a worker's Threshold, Floor, Share, Every Ticks, Tick Pause or Required Weight, at the entity's or the change's. A level, a pointer's button, an order, a priority among configs and a log's size stay whole numbers, as do a kind's and a scaler's own numbers. A folded field's line shows a number field by what it reads, as "Uses 3".

  • Constant: the field reads its number, with its unit beside it, as Tick Every's "1 s". A field that used to read its scaler, and has none, reads 0, and a warning says so.

  • Table: the field reads one value for each level, exactly: level 1 the first, level 4 the 4th, and a level past the last the last, as Laguna Blade's 450, 600 and 750. The range it gives shows beside the kind, as "6–8 s", or "-25 to -10" where it goes below 0. Its values sit in a row under it, with no curve.

  • By Level: the field reads its values spread over a level range, as points on a curve, a level between two reading between them (below). It's the same number by level as a Table, read another way, so choosing one for the other keeps its values; one saved reading exactly shows as a Table, and nothing saved changes. SPREADSHEETS.md writes a project's Tables and By Levels to a spreadsheet and reads them back.

  • Shared: the field reads a Scaler asset (below).

  • The other kinds work out a value from the values or from the entity: × Attribute reads an attribute of the caster or the target, its current, its base, what's missing of it (base − current) or its share of its base (current ÷ base), which the menu starts as the field's number times it, all of it or only some of its retention groups (below), and reads it as its attribute's role says, a Bonus Share as 1 + x and a Resistance as 1 − x, or as its Read As says, Clamped keeps a number within bounds, Conditional reads one number where a condition holds and another where it doesn't, Randomized rolls a random value, Threshold steps at thresholds, and Fixed Value is one number. Stacking Curve turns a Count, as an item's stacks or an attribute, into its number by a Curve and its Coefficient, a: Linear, a × n, as 15% a stack; Hyperbolic, 1 − 1 / (1 + a × n), a chance that nears 1 and never reaches it, 13% with one stack and 60% with ten at 0.15; Reciprocal, 1 / (1 + a × n), as what Dota's armor leaves of a hit at 0.06; Inverse, a / n, as a wind-up an attack speed shortens; and Exponential, a to the n. Its Plus is added after, 1 making a share a multiplier. Effect Stacks reads the stack count of the effect's own container, and Item Stacks that of the item that applied it (ITEMS.md); each reads 1 where there's nothing stacked. An attribute set's values use the cached kinds, which read other attributes as the entity's values change.

  • Set By Caller reads a value the code passes in. It checks one thing: whether what the number is read in holds a value under exactly its Key. Code sets one on an effect's spec before applying it (spec.SetByCaller(tag, value)), on an ability activation's data (data.SetByCaller(tag, value)), which each effect the ability makes takes and the ability's tasks read, or on the data that spawns a process (EFFECTS.md). Only the key itself matches, not a tag above or below it in the tag tree, and a value set to 0 is a value. Where none is set, the field reads the kind's Default; its summary says both, as "the caller's Damage, else 0".

  • A Threshold lists its thresholds, each a Level and an optional Tier name, with the Value it reads from that level up under them, a number of any kind; below every threshold it reads its Default Value.

  • A Conditional has a condition, When, and two numbers, True Value and False Value: it reads True where the condition holds, and False where it doesn't, or where it has none. Its summary says all three, as "−0.1 × Target's Health (base) if Target's Health < 0.3 × Target's Health (base), else 0". A condition picks its kind from a menu, as a number does, and says itself in a line:

    • Tag: whether the caster or the target holds a tag, counted by its weight, so a tag granted twice counts twice;
    • Attribute: an attribute of the caster or the target, its current, its base, what's missing of it or its share of its base, compared with a number, as 0.3 × the target's base Health, or a share above 0.9 for "above 90%";
    • Level: the level, or how far up its range it is, from 0 to 1, compared with a number;
    • Compare Numbers: one number compared with another;
    • Always and Never;
    • and under Logic, All Of, Any Of and Not, which join others. All Of none holds; Any Of none doesn't.

    Where what the number is read in lacks what a condition reads, as a target's tag where there's no target, the condition doesn't hold, and neither does Not it.

  • A Clamped keeps its Value between its Min Value and Max Value, three numbers of any kind; one made in the inspector clamps a value by level.

  • A Randomized scaler's Variance is a kind of its own: Percentage, up or down by a Share of its value; Flat, by an Amount; Multiplier, times one between its Min and its Max; or one of your own, as two dice, a [Serializable] class made from AbstractVariance that overrides Vary, Lowest and Highest. Its numbers read the variance as their object, so a Share can widen by level and a Max can be 3 × its Min.

  • A scaler's own numbers read the scaler as their object: a Clamped's Max Value can be Relative to a Field, 2 × its Min Value, whatever field the Clamped is in.

  • A Group joins several numbers as one. It lists its members, each an Operation then its Scaler, which can be any kind. The members that Add are added up, that sum is multiplied by each member that Multiplies and divided by each that Divides, one that reads 0 dividing nothing, then each Flat Bonus is added on, in whatever order they're listed, so (5 + Caster's Strength) × 2 + 3 is one number, and 1.7 ÷ Caster's Attack Speed another, and a first member needn't add. Members that multiply or divide with none that adds work on 0, and the Group says so. A member that Overrides gives the group's value alone; where several do, the group's policy picks the highest, the lowest, their average, the first or the last, and Ignore sets them aside. A Group has no values per level. With no members, or none that holds a kind, it's no kind at all, so its field reads its number, as a Constant does.

Kinds that read the game, each with a summary that says what it reads:

  • Local Data reads the number under a Key in Local Data: Its Own, what the number belongs to, as its ability's or its item's asset, or the Caster's or the Target's as the entity holds it. Where there's no number under the key it reads its Default. A hero's attack range kept in its Local Data is one field read by every ability. The entry's number can scale too, read where the kind reads it: Its Own in the number's context, the Caster's or the Target's at that entity's own level. One that reads its own key stops at the Default.
  • Relative to a Field reads another number field of the same object, picked from a menu of them, and the field's number joins it as Combine says: a Clamped scaler's Max can be 2 × its Min, and a stack bound's Max its Min + 3. The other field is read in the same context, so a relative Max follows a Min that scales by level. A field offers this kind where its code hands its object over as it reads (below).
  • Tracked Impact, under Effect, reads what an effect has dealt: its Last impact, its Total, or its impact Count. Whose: This Effect, as a burn that grows by its own tick count; An Effect on the caster or the target, by its asset; or Effects Tagged there, added up. An impact is as it landed, after the target's workers, and signed, so damage is negative; By Size reads it positive, as a heal of half a blow wants. Before an effect's first impact, and after a load, it reads 0.
  • Group Impact reads one retention group's part of an attribute on the caster or the target, as "Caster's Strength (Bonus)": the Strength items add, and none of the hero's own.
  • Retention groups in any attribute read: × Attribute, the Attribute condition, a relative attribute worker's Relative To and the Threshold worker each have Groups: All, the whole value, as before; Only These, as a bonus of the items' Strength reads their group; or All But These, as a bonus of a hero's own damage leaves its items' out. A group names the groups under it too, so Items takes Items.Ring. The read says which: "Caster's Strength (Items)", "Caster's Attack Damage (all but Items)".
  • Asset Part finds effects, abilities or items on the caster or the target by a tag, and reads a part of them. An effect matches by its asset tag, a context tag or a tag it grants, and an ability or an item by its asset tag or a context tag; a tag under the one given matches too.
    • Of an Effect: its Count, Stacks, Level, Last Impact, Total Impact, Impact Count, Duration Remaining, Duration, Time Until Tick, or a Worker Value, what that effect's workers answer under a key.
    • Of an Ability: its Count, Level, Is Active, Is on Cooldown, or Cooldown Remaining.
    • Of an Item: its Count, Stacks, Level, or Is Equipped.
    • Among says how several that match read as one: their Sum, the First, the Highest or the Lowest. A true-or-false part reads 1 or 0, so its sum is how many are so. Where none matches, a count reads 0 and any other part the Default.
  • Worker asks workers for a number under a Key: This Effect's own workers, or the Caster's or the Target's. Where none answers it reads its Default. See Asking a worker, below.

Choosing a kind is one Undo step. A constant made a Table or By Level keeps its number as its first level's value.

With more than one asset selected, the field edits each asset's own field. Kinds that differ show as mixed, and choosing one gives it to each field, from that field's own value; a field of that kind keeps its scaler. Levels and Make Constant work on each field's own values, and a count, range or curve the fields disagree on shows as mixed. Save as Scaler Asset waits for one asset.

A kind other than Constant shows a one-line summary of what the field reads under its row, as "0.3 × Target's Health (base)", then Combine, how the field joins its number with the scaler: Scaler reads the scaler's value, Scaler + Number and Scaler × Number add or multiply the field's number, and Number Only reads the number. A whole-number field, such as Ticks, also shows its Rounding: Floor, Ceil, or Round, to the nearest, a half away from zero. A field whose unit is "%" stores 0.3 and shows and takes it as 30%. A kind its field can't work out, as one copied from a field that has a target into one that hasn't, says so under the field, and the Problems window lists it; it reads the field's number.

The field menu holds the items below. A scaler field of its own, as a Group member's, has the same menu, and its kind menu files its kinds by category, as a number field's does.

  • Save as Scaler Asset…: a name and a folder, then Save and Use, which saves the field's scaler as a Scaler asset and makes the field share it.
  • Scaler Assets: a submenu of the project's Scaler assets, the one the field shares checked. The field shares the one you pick.
  • Import Values…: a window that lists the Scaler assets and the other assets' fields with values per level. Pick one to compare its values with the field's, level by level, with both curves, then Copy Values. A shorter table's last value holds for the levels past its end, and the window says which levels those are. One Undo takes it back.
  • Quick Fill…: fills the table from a pattern: Same Value, Straight Line, Slow Then Fast and Fast Then Slow with a Strength, Steps, Add Each Level, Multiply Each Level, Ease In and Out, or a Custom Curve you draw, from one value to another. Rounding makes the values whole numbers or rounds them to a Step. A preview shows the values as the table will, with their curve, and one Undo takes them back. On a scaler spread over levels it fills the points, as many as Points says, and the preview shows what each level reads between them.
  • Make Constant: the field reads a number again, the value its first level read. A scaler field of its own, with no number, becomes a Fixed Value of that value.

Values per level

A scaler's settings sit on a shaded block under its field, so they read as the scaler's among the fields around them. A kind shows only what it reads: a Fixed Value shows its value, and a Randomized scaler its variance, then the values its base value comes from. A kind's field that applies only to some of its settings shows only for them, as an Asset Part's worker key for an effect's Worker Value.

  • Values: Per Level or Spread Over Levels. A Table reads Per Level and By Level spreads, as their kinds say, so they have no choice here; a scaler of another kind with values for each level chooses.
    • Per Level: one value per level, ten levels to a row. Level 1 reads the first value, level 4 the 4th, and a level past the end reads the last.
    • Spread Over Levels: the values are points on a curve over a level range, and a level between two points reads between them. Three values, 10, 20 and 30, spread over levels 1 to 5 read 10, 15, 20, 25 and 30. The points are numbered, and under them each level shows what it reads. A scaler you make in the inspector starts Per Level. The scalers saved before the choice existed spread, so they read as they did.
  • Levels, Per Level: an asset with a level provider, as an ability, or an effect linked to one, follows the provider's levels, as Fireball's levels (1–4), with a value for each. A level past the scaler's values shows the last in secondary text; type there to give it a value of its own. Own levels gives the scaler a count of its own. An asset whose levels come from whatever uses it, as an unlinked effect, an attribute set or a Scaler asset, shows the count alone. A new level takes the last value.
  • Over Levels, spread: the provider's levels, Own levels up to the scaler's own Max Level, or the provider's up to a level: its levels, up to the Max Level, so the values reach the last at the lower of the Max Level and the provider's last level, and a level past it reads the last value. Points: how many values.
  • Between Points, spread: Steps, Straight Line or Smooth Curve.
  • A read-only curve beside the table shows the values, and spread, the curve they make. Its tooltip reads them, as "6 at level 1, rising to 8 at level 5".
  • Behaviours: rules that adjust the value the scaler works out, in order.

Quick Fill fills the levels the table shows. Nothing changes a scaler's values while it's drawn: its table keeps its length until you change it.

Writing your own scaler

A scaler of your own derives from AbstractScaler, or from AbstractCachedScaler for an attribute set's values, and has a public constructor with no parameters. The Kind menu lists it by its name without "Scaler", as Damage Curve for DamageCurveScaler. Its inspector draws:

  • Each field it adds, public or [SerializeField], as Unity draws the field's type, through any property drawer you write for that type. The fields of an abstract base of your own draw too. [HideInInspector] keeps a field out, and [ForgeShowWhen(nameof(Mode), EMode.Curved)] shows one only while another field, an enum or a bool, holds one of the values given.
  • The parts every scaler has, as its Parts say: its values per level (EScalerParts.LevelValues), the level range they spread over (EScalerParts.LevelRange), and its behaviours (EScalerParts.Behaviours). Unless you override it, a scaler reads all three, or its behaviours alone where its UseScalingOptions() returns false. Override Parts to say what yours reads, as a scaler that reads only an attribute returns EScalerParts.Behaviours. Parts can follow the scaler's own fields, as one that reads its values per level in one mode alone.

A kind of number of your own is simpler: derive from NumberKind and override its one method, Evaluate(in NumberContext context), which reads what the field's site has: context.Caster, context.Target, context.Level, the caller's magnitudes, Local Data, an attribute on either side, and more; context.Has(...) says which. Describe() gives the field's summary, as "the distance to the target". [NumberKind("Distance to Target", Reads = NumberReads.Caster | NumberReads.Target)] names it and says what it reads, so a field offers it only where it can be worked out; where it can't, the field reads its number. [ForgeMenu("Space")] files it in the kind menu. A kind is a scaler, so it also works in an effect's magnitude and inside a Group or a Conditional, and its fields draw as a scaler's do. Test one without an effect: number.Evaluate(NumberContext.For(caster, target, level)). A kind is saved by its class's name, so renaming the class needs Unity's [MovedFrom]. PlayForge's own kinds, Local Data, Tracked Impact, Asset Part and the rest, are written this way, in Runtime/PlayForge/Numbers/Kinds.

A condition of your own, for a Conditional, is the same: derive from GameplayCondition, override Evaluate(in NumberContext context) to say whether it holds and Describe() for its line, and name it with [GameplayCondition("Target Is Caster", Reads = NumberReads.Caster | NumberReads.Target)]; the condition menu offers it, and condition.Holds(context) reads it. A kind or a condition that holds numbers reads them with itself as their owner, MaxValue.Evaluate(context, this), and passes Initialize(carrier) on to them, so a number in it captures what it captures as an effect's spec is made. PlayForge's are in Runtime/PlayForge/Numbers/Conditions.

A number field in your own code

A field of your own is one line, and a float converts to it:

[Tooltip("How far it reaches")]
[NumberContext(NumberReads.Level | NumberReads.Caster, Unit = "m")]
[NumberMin(0)]
public ScalableFloat Range = 20f;
  • Read it with Range.Evaluate(context), with a NumberContext built Of what the code has: an effect's spec, an activation's data, a worker's context, an entity, a cue or a process. Range.Evaluate(context, this) also hands the field's object over, which is what Relative to a Field reads; a field read that way declares NumberReads.Owner, or NumberReads.All, and its kind menu then offers that kind.
  • [Tooltip] shows on the field's name.
  • [NumberMin(0)], [NumberMax(10)] and [NumberRange(0, 1)] limit the constant typed in the inspector, and a range sets it on a slider. On a field whose unit is "%" the limit is in what the field stores, so [NumberRange(0, 1)] is 0% to 100%, and a whole-number field takes the whole numbers inside the limits. They limit the constant alone: a number of another kind is worked out as the game runs, so where what it reads must stay inside limits, give it the Clamped kind or clamp what the code reads.
  • In a kind of your own, as an ability task, a targeting task or a scaler, [ShownWhen(nameof(UsePhysics))] shows a field only while a toggle of the same object is on, and [ShownWhen(nameof(Mode), (int)EMode.Area)] only while an enum is one of the values, as a targeting task's Detection Layers show under Use Physics. A task reads its numbers with Read(number, data), at its ability's level.
  • Every kind of your own, a task, a worker, a component, a cue, a kind of number or a condition, takes [ForgeMenu("Path/Sub", Name = "...", Description = "...")]: its picker files it under the submenus its path names, a slash nesting them; Name replaces its class's name in the picker and in its field; and Description is what the picker's Context says while it's highlighted. A path holds for the classes made from the one that names it, and [ForgeMenu("")] puts a kind at the top whatever its base names.
  • Unity's own [Min] and [Range] don't work on a number field: Unity gives the field to that attribute's drawer, which draws "Use Range with float or int" where the number was. As scripts load, the console names each number field that has one, with the attribute to use in its place.

Asking a worker

A number can't count or remember; a worker can. So a number of the Worker kind asks workers for a value under a key, and any worker answers by implementing INumberWorker:

public class OverkillWorker : AbstractEffectWorker, INumberWorker
{
    public Tag Key;

    public bool TryGetNumber(Tag key, in NumberContext context, out float value)
    {
        value = 0f;
        if (key != Key) return false;   // not a key it knows: the next worker is asked
        var state = (context.Carrier as GameplayEffectSpec)?.GetWorkerState<State>(this);
        value = state?.Overkill ?? 0f;
        return true;
    }
}
  • An effect's workers answer about one application of their effect. A worker is one object on its effect's asset, shared by every application, so what it counts for each is kept on the spec: ctx.GetState<State>(this) in its events, and spec.GetWorkerState<State>(this) as it answers.
  • An entity's workers, those its worker groups give it, answer about the entity. A worker group installs the ones that answer as it's given, and code that adds a worker itself calls NumberWorkers.Provide(entity, worker).
  • The first worker that answers the key gives the value. Where none does, the number reads its Default.
  • Any number field can ask: an effect's magnitude, a task's range, a threshold. Code can ask too, with NumberWorkers.TryAsk. An Asset Part's Worker Value asks the workers of each effect it finds.

The Attribute Change effect worker is one that answers. It notes an attribute as each application of its effect begins, and answers how much the attribute has Lost, Gained or Changed since, or what it was At Start. With Every Ticks above 0 it answers only on each Nth tick, and 0 on the others. A curse that deals 5 a second, and every fourth second a quarter of the health its target has lost since it landed, from every source, is one periodic effect: its first impact is −5, and its second is a Worker number, −0.25 × the worker's answer, with the worker watching the target's Health, Lost, Every Ticks 4.

Scaler assets

A Scaler asset (Create → PlayForge → Scaler) holds one scaler that many fields can use. A field of the Shared kind reads its Asset's scaler, so an edit to the asset changes every field that uses it, with nothing to copy or propagate. Open shows the asset in a window of its own. Under the field, the asset's curve shows beside its range, as "6–8, levels 1–5", and how many fields share it. A field adjusts the shared values only through its own number and Combine: Scaler × Number with a number of 1.5 reads the asset's values times 1.5, as in Fire Duration × 1.5.

  • The asset's own inspector shows its name, its kind and how many fields use it, its Name, which renames it, as The Forge and the field menus list it, its Description, its Values, and Used By: each field that shares it, by its asset and field. Click a row to select the asset. In The Forge, its summary says its description, its kind, what it reads and how many fields use it.
  • Its Try section reads it at each level of a range, Levels 1 to 5 to start, as an effect's Levels does: a row of what it gives at each, and under it the parts behind the level picked. It reads against a stand-in Caster and Target and a Caller, each with a row for what the scaler asks of it, found as it reads: an attribute on its side, whether the side Has it, its current and its base; and a key the caller sets, whether it's Set and its value, unset reading the scaler's default. A side's Entity fills its attributes from what that entity's attribute set starts each at, at its starting level, and one the set doesn't give, it lacks. A stand-in holds attributes alone, so a number that reads an effect, an item, a tag, a retention group's part or Local Data reads as on an entity with none of them. What's typed is kept for the session, never in the asset. ScalerTry does the same in code: new ScalerTry { From = 1, To = 5 }.Read(scaler).
  • Any magnitude field can share a Scaler asset. An attribute set's element shares one whose scaler is a cached kind, and its Scaler Assets lists only those. A Scaler asset can't hold a Shared scaler of its own.
  • A field whose asset is missing reads as a field with no scaler, and an element whose asset is missing reads its root value. A chain of shared scalers that comes back to its own asset stops after eight steps, rather than run forever.

The Scaler asset was the Scaler Template, whose link and sync settings nothing read, so those settings are gone. So are the old scaler window's curve editor and import window: a scaler's values are its table, and Import Values compares them before it copies.
