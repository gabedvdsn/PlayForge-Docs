PlayForge — Cues

A cue is what something shows and sounds, apart from what it changes: a spark as a hit lands, a hum while a shield holds, a shake as a giant falls. Effects, abilities, entities and items each list their cues, and a game's own code raises one where it likes. Every cue plays through ForgeCues, after Unreal's gameplay cues: a burst at a moment, as a hit's spark, or a lasting cue's beginning and its end, as a shield's hum, which share a key.

Where cues are raised

  • Effects: an effect's Cues component plays its cues as it lands, as an application it takes in lands, as each tick executes, as an impact it deals lands, as it comes off, while it lasts, as an application is refused and as its stacks change (EFFECTS.md).
  • Abilities: a Cues component plays an ability's as it activates, as it commits, while it's active, as it ends and as one is refused; a Play Cue task plays them at a stage's exact moment, on the caster, on the target or at the point (ABILITIES.md).
  • Entities: a Cues component plays an entity's as it spawns, dies and comes back, and lasting ones while it holds a tag, as stars over its head while it's Stunned (ENTITIES.md).
  • Items: a Cues component plays an item's as it's equipped, while it's equipped and as it's used (ITEMS.md).
  • Code: ForgeCues.Burst(sparks, new CueContext(entity, ECueMoment.Raised, point: hit.point)) plays sparks where a ray hit, and ForgeCues.Begin and ForgeCues.End begin and end a lasting one.

A cue's context, CueContext, says what raised it, its Moment, as Applied, Activated or Died; its target and its source; the effect or the ability behind it; where it plays, its Point and Normal, or else its target's place; a magnitude; and, for a lasting cue, the key its beginning and its end share.

Handlers

Each cue in a list is a handler, its kind picked from the list. It plays at each moment whose list holds it, and, in a list of lasting cues, as a While Active, from its beginning to its end:

  • Camera Shake shakes the camera through the shaker the game gives ForgeCues.Shaker, an ICameraShaker.
  • Sound plays its clip where its Placement says (Placement).
  • Prefab places its prefab where its Placement says, for its Lifetime, or, as a lasting cue, until its cue ends (Pooling).
  • Floating Text floats a text where its Placement says, as a hit's damage, the gold a kill gave or a status's name (Floating text).
  • Process runs a MonoBehaviour process where its Placement says, on a packet loaded for the cue, with its Data list last (Data packets): a burst's runs its own course, and a lasting one's runs until its cue ends (Pooling).
  • Sequence runs a task sequence on a packet loaded for the cue, with its Data list last: a burst's runs its course, and a lasting one's runs until its cue ends. It goes by the clock its Clock picks, else the sequence's own, else that of the one it plays on.
  • Cue Tag plays what its tag routes to (Routes).

Write a handler of your own by deriving from AbstractCueHandler: override the moments it plays at, or Burst to play every burst one way, reading the moment from cue.Moment, and Begin and End for a lasting one.

Placement

A cue that places something, a Prefab or a Sound, says where with its Placement:

  • At: on whom.
    • Self: the one the cue is on: an effect's target, an ability's caster, the entity, or an item's holder. Where its raiser gave a point, as a hit's, it's there.
    • Source: who's behind it, as an effect's source or a death's killer.
    • Target: an ability's first target, or the place it targeted. For another's cue, the one it's on.
    • Each Target: one at each of an ability's targets.
    • Point: the cue's point, or the place an ability targeted.
  • Socket: a child of theirs it's placed at, by name, found at any depth, as a hand or a weapon's tip.
  • Offset moves it, and Scatter moves it by a random amount up to that much either way on each axis, both in the space of what it's placed at.
  • Attach: whether it follows them: a lasting cue's only, by default; always; or never. An attached sound plays from a source of its own under them.
  • Facing: as what it's placed at faces, or along the cue's normal at a point; along the cue's normal; away from or toward its source; or the world's way.

Scatter draws from ForgeCues.Random, the cues' own random numbers, so a cue never moves the game's rolls: a server that plays no cues and a client that plays them must draw alike.

Floating text

A Floating Text cue floats a text where its Placement says, 2 units above the one the cue is on by default: a hit's damage, the gold a kill gave, a status's name as it lands.

  • Text: what it says. {value} is its number, {name} the name of what raised it, as an effect's, and {tag} its cue tag: +{value} gold, or {name} for a status.
  • Value: its number, From the cue's magnitude, as a hit's value from an effect's On Hit, or its Number, read as it plays, as a Tracked Impact's for the gold an instant effect gave. It's written with up to Decimals decimals; without its sign where Absolute says, as damage shown as 35; with a Plus Sign above 0; shortened past a thousand where Abbreviate says, as 1.2k; and not at all for 0 where Hide Zero says.
  • Look: its Color, or its Negative Color below 0 where By Sign says; its Size, Font, Bold and Outline; turned to the camera where Face Camera says, ForgeCues.Camera or else the main camera; and its Sorting Order. A Prefab holding a TextMeshPro text shows it in place of the cue's own, its font, size and outline its own.
  • Motion: its Lifetime; its Velocity, and a Random Velocity either way on each axis; Gravity, for an arc, and Drag; its opacity over its life, Fade, and its scale, Scale, as a pop; on the game's clock where Game Time says, else in real time.
  • Stacking, on one entity: a number Merge Within some seconds of the last adds to it, which shows the sum and floats again, as a burn's ticks; each text floats a Stagger above the one below it, in the lowest place free; and at most Max Shown float at once, the oldest going first.

Its texts come from ForgeCuePool and go back to it. For damage numbers, put one in an effect's On Hit: it plays as each impact the effect deals lands, on the one it lands on, told the value it changed.

Routes

A Cue Tag cue names a tag, as Cue.Hit.Fire, in place of what it shows, so an effect, an ability and an entity can each raise Cue.Hit.Fire and one place says how it looks. That place is the project's Cue Library, an asset from Create > PlayForge > Cue Library, which Project Settings > PlayForge names under Forge Project. Each of its routes is a cue tag and the handlers it plays.

  • A tag plays its own route, or else its nearest parent's: with no route for Cue.Hit.Fire, Cue.Hit's plays.
  • A route that doesn't Override plays its parent's after it, so Cue.Hit.Fire's flames add to Cue.Hit's spark.
  • An entity's Cue Receiver component plays a tag its own way first, in place of the library's, or before it with Pass Through. Its Variant plays the library's route under its name, as Cue.Hit.Golem for Cue.Hit, where the library has one, so a golem's hit sounds of stone.
  • Code can give a library of its own, ForgeCueRoutes.Library, in place of the project's.

Raised

The Cue Library's inspector lists under Raised each cue tag the project's assets raise through it, by a Cue Tag cue, in tag order. Each tag shows what plays it, "plays its own route", "plays Cue.Hit's route" or "plays nowhere", then a row for each asset that raises it, which opens the asset in The Forge. The section's header counts the tags, and how many of them play nowhere. It follows the project as it changes, and the library's routes as they're edited.

The Problems view notes a Cue Tag cue that plays nowhere:

  • one that names no tag;
  • one whose project names no Cue Library;
  • one whose tag the library routes nowhere, neither its own route nor a parent's.

It's a note, not a warning, as an entity's Cue Receiver may play the tag its own way. An edit to the library, or to the library a project names, checks every asset again.

Pooling

A Prefab cue with Pooled takes its objects from ForgeCuePool and gives them back as its cue ends, a burst's after its Lifetime, in place of making and destroying one each time; the pool makes Preallocate of them ahead as the cue first plays. An IPooledCue on the prefab is told as it's taken and as it's given back, to reset it.

A Process cue with Pooled does the same through CuePoolInstantiator, the instantiator its process's registration gives: each object comes from the pool, placed where the cue's packet says, and goes back as its process ends.

Data packets

A cue that runs a process or a sequence hands it a data packet of its own, loaded for what raised the cue, CuePackets.For(cue, data). It loads in four steps, each step's keys taking the place of an earlier step's:

  1. An ability's activation: an ability's cue starts from a copy of its activation's packet, with its targets, its targeting data and the numbers its caller set.
  2. The cue's context:
    • the cue itself under Tags.CUE, a CueContext;
    • its asset under Tags.ASSET, the effect, the ability, the item or the entity's identity, and its spec under Tags.SPEC, the effect's, the ability's or the item's;
    • its source under Tags.SOURCE, and the one it plays on under Tags.TARGET_REAL where the activation named no target;
    • where it plays under Tags.POSITION and Tags.ROTATION, and what it follows under Tags.TRANSFORM;
    • its owner's clock: the activation's for an ability's cue, else that of the one it plays on.
  3. The asset's Local Data: each row under its key, its numbers read for the cue, as at its level.
  4. The cue's own Data list, last, so it has the last word. A row with no value puts nothing.

A process or a sequence reads it as it reads any packet: data.TryGetFirst(Tags.CUE, out CueContext cue).

Raises and sinks

Each raise of a list of cues names what the list belongs to, its CueSource: an effect, an ability, an entity or an item, by its asset; a cue tag raised from code, as ForgeCueRoutes.Burst(cue); or a game's own code, where it names nothing. Code can name one: ForgeCues.Burst(cues, cue, CueSource.Of(effect)).

Before any cue of a raise plays, its world's sink is asked, once: ForgeCueSink.WillPlay(event, raise), the raise holding the cue and its source. The default plays everything. A world of its own takes a sink of its own with ForgeCues.UseSink(world, sink), and ForgeCues.Sink is the one every other world asks. It's asked whatever mutes a cue, so a server that plays none can still send them on, as a network layer's sink does. A cue tag raised from code asks once; its routes, and a Cue Tag within them, play inside that raise, unasked.

An effect's applier can say where it hit, before applying it: spec.WithHit(point, normal, magnitude). Its cues then play at the point, facing the normal, with the magnitude, as a sword's spark where the blade struck.

Silencing and listening

  • ForgeCues.Enabled = false silences every cue, as on a server or while a simulation runs ahead.
  • ForgeCues.Mute(entity, true) silences one entity's, as one off screen. A lasting cue that began still ends.
  • ForgeCues.Played hears each cue as it plays, for a debugger or a recorder.
