PlayForge — Entities

An Entity asset (EntityIdentity) describes one kind of entity, such as the hero or a goblin. An entity is anything that runs a gameplay ability system (GAS). The asset is the entity's starting setup: its attributes, abilities, items, tags, level and workers. Many entities can share one asset.

At runtime a GAS hub reads the asset when it initializes. The hub is either the GameplayAbilitySystem component on a GameObject, or a RuntimeGameplayAbilitySystem made from code. It subscribes the entity's workers, registers its level, gives its Starting Abilities, builds its attributes from its Attribute Set, gives its Starting Items, and then applies its tags.

Tags are names, such as State.Stunned, that an entity carries and that requirements check. Each asset stores a tag made from its Name: Hero gives Entity.Hero. The entity carries this tag and keeps its own level under it. Each section below matches the inspector section of the same name. A collapsed section shows one line about what it holds, such as the level range or how many starting items the entity equips.

The inspector's header shows the entity's one line, as The Forge's Summary column does: its attribute set's first three attributes as it starts, at its starting level, then its starting abilities, as "Health 500, Mana 300, Armor 5; Fireball, Blink".

Identity

  • Name: the entity's display name. It also gives the asset tag, so renaming the entity changes its tag. Update anything that names the old tag.
  • Description: free text for your UI.
  • Asset Tag: read-only, made from the Name: punctuation is dropped, and each word is capitalised and joined. The tag is stored when you edit the Name, and the inspector header shows it after the entity's type. If the stored tag differs from the one the Name gives, it shows in warning text with an Update button that stores the new one.
  • Textures: icons, each paired with a tag. The texture tagged Primary is the entity's icon. Without one, the first texture is used.

Leveling

An entity's level is kept under its asset tag. A level provider is an asset whose level range another asset follows.

  • Link Mode: Standalone (default) keeps the entity's own range. Linked To Provider shows a provider's range instead.
  • Starting Level (default 1): the entity's level when its GAS initializes, and its lowest level.
  • Max Level (default 99): its highest level. The level always stays within this range.
  • Level Provider, while linked: the asset whose range the section shows, an Entity, Ability, Effect, Item or Attribute Set. Right-click the field to pick from one type. A link that would make a loop is refused, with an error in the Console. While no provider is set, a warning says the entity uses its own range.
  • Link Items & Abilities links this entity's Starting Items and Starting Abilities to it, so their per-level values follow its range. Unlink Items & Abilities removes the links that point at this entity. Each asks first, and one Undo takes it back.

The entity's level feeds per-level values: the effects it applies as an origin, its Max Abilities and item limits, and the cached scalers in its attribute set. Code changes the level through the GAS, with SetLevel or ModifyLevel and the entity's asset tag. At runtime, the entity's level range comes from its own Starting Level and Max Level, even while it is linked.

Tags

  • Affiliation: the entity's team or faction tags. An effect's Affiliation check compares its origin's affiliation with its target's. Code that starts a GAS can pass affiliation tags in its registration data, and those replace this list; as the game runs, SetAffiliation on either hub sets them, as a side switch or a scripted betrayal. The GAS also applies these tags to the entity, on either hub. An effect's Side Change goes over them while it lasts (EFFECTS.md), and GetSides says which it's under.
  • Context Tags describe the entity, such as Undead. Changes the entity makes as an origin itself carry them as context, and workers can filter by them.
  • Granted Tags are always applied to the entity.

Code hears an entity's tags change through its tag cache. gas.GetTagCache().Listen(tag, onChange) hears the tag, and every tag under it, come and go, as it happens; pass ETagChangeKind.AnyCount to hear every change to a tag's weight. Each change says the tag and its weight before and after. When the entity rebuilds its tags from their sources, as it does when an ability starts or ends, an item changes or a loose tag is granted, it tells only what the rebuild changed. Dispose the listener to stop it. TagChanged hears every change to any tag.

var listener = gas.GetTagCache().Listen(stunned, change => animator.SetBool("Stunned", change.Added));
listener.Dispose();

A tag worker is evaluated at the end of a frame in which the entity's tags changed. One whose ValidateWorkFor reads more than the tags overrides EvaluateEveryFrame to be evaluated every frame.

Attributes

The entity's attributes come from its Attribute Set. Under the field, a table lists each attribute the set gives, its sub-sets' included, with the value it starts at and its range. When the GAS initializes, it creates every attribute in the set and its sub-sets, each at its starting Current and Base values. It also applies the set's asset tags to the entity and subscribes the set's workers. Gameplay effects then change the values. ATTRIBUTE_SETS.md describes the set itself.

An entity with no Attribute Set has no attributes. An effect that targets an attribute the entity lacks leaves it unchanged.

Time Scale names the attribute the entity's time runs at, 1 as the game's. Its effects, cooldowns, sequences and waits run by that time, so an effect that lowers the attribute slows the entity, one that raises it hastes it, and as each comes off the entity's time is the game's again. Give the attribute a Current of 1 in the set. Code sets a scale of its own, GetClock().TimeScale, as a hit-stop's 0, and the clock reads the two multiplied, so a hit-stop holds under a slow; OwnScale is code's part and AttributeScale the attribute's.

Abilities

  • When Busy (default Queue): what the entity's abilities set to Entity's Policy do when one is asked to activate while another is busy. Activate Anyway starts it at once. Refuse starts it only when no ability under that choice is busy, and refuses it otherwise. Queue makes it wait in a queue while one is busy. ABILITIES.md explains each.
  • Starting Abilities: abilities the entity learns when its GAS initializes, each at its own Starting Level. Those set to Activate At Start then try to activate.
  • Max Abilities (default 99): the most abilities the entity can hold. Its kind can make it a scaler, which changes the limit with the entity's level (SCALERS.md).
  • Allow Duplicates (default off): let the entity hold the same ability more than once.
  • Crowd Control: the crowd control rules its abilities follow, as a stun stops every ability and Silenced spells, or, where it names none, the project's (ABILITIES.md). A runtime entity's identity names them too.

Items

  • Starting Items: items the entity is given when its GAS initializes, one row each. Equip equips the item as it is given.
  • Max Items (default 99): the most items the inventory can hold.
  • Max Equipped Items (default 99): the most items equipped at once.
  • Allow Duplicates (default off): let the inventory hold the same item more than once.
  • Equipped Too (default off): let more than one copy of the same item be equipped at once. It needs Allow Duplicates, since an inventory that holds one of each item has no second copy to equip.

Each Max field is a number, or, by its kind, a scaler that changes the limit with the entity's level, with its rounding (SCALERS.md). A give or equip that breaks a limit fails.

Components

An entity's components are features it carries, each a class with hooks into its life, added with the list's +. A component hears the entity Register, its systems set up; Death, whether it stays or ends, its context's Killer the one whose loss killed it, as its Died event names; Revive, for one that stays; and End. It's waited on as one of its abilities' activations winds up, WindUp, before the ability's cost and first stage, as a turn to face its target is. A kind that makes no sense twice is offered only while the entity holds none of it.

A component of your own derives from AbstractEntityComponent and overrides the hooks it needs, each a no-op otherwise. It's the entity's, shared by everything that holds it, so what it keeps for one entity it keeps through its context: ctx.GetState<T>(this). Its context's Identity is the entity's settings, its Death among them.

Services

A service is a per-entity object an entity's components share, as the auras it runs, AuraSet, and the wait a Respawn counts down, RespawnTimer. One is made the first time it's asked for, gas.GetService<T>(), and found without making one, gas.FindService<T>(). The entity steps each by its own time each frame, Step, after its effects and before its turn and its barriers; tells each as it revives, Revive, before anything else of the revive; and ends each as it ends, End. A service of your own derives from AbstractEntityService and overrides what it needs, as a heat meter that cools by the entity's time; a save keeps none.

Auras

An Aura component holds an effect on those near the entity as long as it lives, as a captain's command aura (EFFECTS.md). An entity that dies takes its auras' effects off, and one that stays and revives holds them again.

Bounty

A Bounty component gives the entity's killer an Effect as it dies, from the entity, so the effect reads its level, as gold or experience. With a Share Radius, read at its level, the killer's allies within it of the entity take the effect too, as an assist, measured along the ground unless Flat is off. A death no one dealt gives none. An entity holds as many as it gives.

Turns To Face

A Turns To Face component turns the entity to face the target, or the point, of each of its abilities before the ability's first stage: at its Turn Speed, in degrees a second of its own time, a number that can read a turn rate attribute, 0 facing at once. The stage starts once it faces within the Start Angle, and the turn goes on to the Facing Angle after, even after the ability has ended, until another turn, its death or its game's word ends it. Where reaching the Start Angle takes longer than Max Wait, If Too Long says what happens: No Limit, the default, waits as long as it takes; Start Anyway starts the stage, the turn going on; Cancel cancels the activation. Its cost and cooldown are paid after the turn, so an interrupted turn pays nothing, and a turn taken over before the stage starts, by another order or its game's word, cancels the activation. An ability with nothing to face, or with the entity for its target, turns nothing; one a trigger sets off turns as any does, as a counterattack turns to its attacker; and one with a Turn To Face of its own turns its own way, or not at all (ABILITIES.md). An entity holds one at most.

Code turns an entity through its hub: gas.Face(target, degreesPerSecond, within), which follows the target as it moves, or gas.Face(point, ...); gas.StopTurning(), as a move of the game's takes over; and gas.AngleToFace, how far it has yet to turn. A turn sets the entity's facing, flat: its object's rotation, or a runtime entity's. A game that turns its characters itself, by animation or a controller, puts an IForgeTurner on the entity's object, which takes each step of a turn in PlayForge's place. The Creator Lab's units stop a turn as they walk or stop.

Respawn

A Respawn component revives an entity that stays as it dies once its Delay, in seconds of its own time and read at its level, has passed, at its Home, where it stood as it registered, Where It Died, a Point, or a place of your own, as a checkpoint, a [Serializable] class made from AbstractRespawnPlace that says Where from the entity's context and its home, as a hero comes back at its fountain. Its Death's On Revive effects land as it does, as any revive's do, and a revive before its time, as a buyback, ends the wait. The wait is the entity's RespawnTimer, a service: a HUD reads gas.FindService<RespawnTimer>()'s Waiting and TimeLeft. An entity that ends as it dies has nothing to revive, which the Problems view says. A save keeps neither the entity's death nor its wait. An entity holds one at most.

Cues

A Cues component plays what the entity shows and sounds at its moments, on it: On Spawn as it comes into the game, On Death as it dies, its killer behind the cue, and On Revive as it comes back. While Tagged lists lasting cues, each under a tag, that play for as long as the entity holds the tag, as stars over its head while it's Stunned: each step, a tag it has taken on begins its cues and one it has let go of ends them, and they end with the entity. Each list holds cues as an effect's does (EFFECTS.md), a Cue Tag among them. A Cue Receiver component plays cue tags its own way for the entity, before the project's cue library. An entity holds one of each at most.

Workers

Workers are reusable behaviours, picked from a list, that react to what happens to an entity. This group is subscribed when the entity's GAS initializes, and stays for the entity's lifetime. The attribute set's workers are subscribed at the same time. Name labels the group. It holds four lists:

  • Attribute workers run before or after an attribute changes, for example to reduce incoming damage.
  • Impact workers inspect the changes the entity deals.
  • Tag workers react when the entity's tags change.
  • Analysis workers examine the frame at its end, for example to detect death.

Each list's + adds an empty worker to pick a kind for. The section's import button copies a worker group from another asset.

A Threshold, an analysis worker, acts as an attribute crosses a value, without code: Megaera rages as her Health falls past half, and Ice Blast shatters below 13%. It reads its Target attribute's What: its current, its base, what's missing of it, or its share of its base, so a Threshold of 0.5 on its share is half. Crossing says when it fires: while its Comparison holds, as it always did; or as what it reads falls to the threshold or below, rises above it, or either. Trigger Once fires at its first crossing alone; off, each time. As it fires it applies its Effects to the entity, from the entity itself, or, with Applied By set to Crosser, from the one whose change crossed it this frame, so a shatter is its caster's, kill and all; and it sends its Event, a gameplay event its abilities' triggers hear, its source the one who crossed it and its subject the entity. It checks at the frame's end, as the death watcher, a Threshold that kills, does, so the frame's hits and heals net out. An entity that revives re-arms it, and its first check after notes its side again.

Two attribute workers keep the common rules. Can't Fall Below holds its attribute at its Floor: a loss that would take it lower takes it to the floor, as Shallow Grave keeps its target at 1 Health, in a buff's worker group while it lasts. Return Damage sends its Share of each workable loss its attribute takes from another back to the one who dealt it, typed Returned, so a return on the other side doesn't send it back again and no lifesteal heals from it, as Blade Mail.

Modifiers, above the workers, change other effects as they're made for as long as the entity lives: its own, as a Frost Mage's every Chill granting Frozen, or those landing on it. Each entry is a rule of its own or names a shared Effect Modifier asset (EFFECTS.md).

An entity's own lifesteal is its Lifesteal, the attribute its project's Lifesteal worker names as its Share, its set's value for it, which the Lifesteal worker heals by (WORKERS.md); its Lifesteal list is gone.

Death

What the entity's death does. Policy is End by default: as it dies, its hub ends it at its frame's end, as an entity always did. Stay keeps it, dead, until it revives: a MOBA hero, or anyone who dies and comes back.

An entity dies as a death watcher among its analysis workers, or its attribute set's, says it does. Where none watches and an attribute plays its project's Health role (ATTRIBUTES.md), it dies as that attribute reaches 0. Dies At Zero Health, on by default, says it does; off, as for a training dummy, that attribute doesn't kill it.

  • A staying entity dies at its frame's end and stays registered, with its items, levels and lasting effects. Its abilities are refused while it's dead.
  • Purges, on by default: its death purges its effects, all but those it keeps, as the games' deaths do (EFFECTS.md). It keeps the categories it keeps whatever their effects' tiers: an ability's Cooldown, what's Lent (by an equipped item, a set bonus, an aura, or a passive, an ability that activates as it's granted, or claimed so by what applied it, as spec.Claimed), a Toggle (a Toggle component's effect, or one an ability's component claims as a toggle's), and its Own, the rest it applied to itself; and the tiers it keeps. Keeps As Project, on, keeps what its project's Purge Levels keep on death, each tier's Kept On Death and their Kept On Death categories, cooldowns, lent effects and toggles as a project starts, and those with no project; Keeps Tiers and Keeps Categories keep more. An effect at no tier goes, unless a category it's of is kept. It took the place of Ends Effects Tagged, which ended effects by their tags.
  • On Death: the effects it takes as it dies. A respawn timer is a durational effect with a Revive On Removal worker: as it ends, the entity revives, where it was or at a point.
  • On Revive: the effects it takes as it revives, as its health back.
  • Body: a MonoBehaviour process, a prefab, that it spawns where it is as it starts and as it revives, and ends as it dies. A runtime entity takes its position from its body, so an area search finds it there; GetBody() returns it. A running entity can be given a body of its own at runtime with UseBody(process), a process running already or registered for it there, which it follows and which ends with it as it dies, as its identity's Body does; a revive spawns the identity's Body, where it has one. The body it had ends first, UseBody(null) lets one go, ending it, and a dead entity takes none.

A dead entity takes no one else's effects, as the dead in the games are untargetable: its own still land, as its On Death respawn timer and its On Revive effects, and an effect another applies to it while it's dead is refused.

Revive(at) on either hub brings a staying entity back, at a point or where it was; it re-arms the entity's analysis workers, so its death watcher sees its next death. Callbacks hear OnEntityRevived, as they hear OnEntityDied.

Combat Log

Each entity keeps a combat log of what happens to it, which a test reads as the game does, through gas.GetCombatLog(), and the Debugger shows under the entity it has selected. What it writes is the project's to say, under Combat Log in Project Settings → PlayForge: its Records, the Hits Taken (a workable change another's effect makes to its attributes), the Hits Dealt, and its other Changes (its own, a cost, regeneration, a transfer); and its Events, the gameplay events it's sent by tag, or one under it, as Died, Kill or your own, written though no ability listens for them. Keep is how many entries each entity keeps, its last, 100 by default; 0 keeps none, as for a build.

Each entry is a CombatLogEntry: its Kind (Taken, Dealt, Changed or Event), its Time and Frame, the Other entity (the dealer of a hit taken, the one a hit dealt struck, an event's source or the one it names), the Effect behind it, the Attribute, its value Before and After and the Change, the hit's Steps through its pipeline, and whether it Killed, marked on the blow that killed in both logs. The log's Entries run oldest first; Of(kind), By(effect) and With(entity) filter them, and Total(kind, attribute) adds their changes up, as the damage an entity took. An entry reads as a line: "Took -20 Health from Lina (Fireball)".

An entry tells how its change came to land, too: its change as Made, before any step, and its ChangeSteps, each step it took on the way: each attribute worker that heard it, with its Timing (before the commit or after), its Execution (inline, or the actions it Queued) and the change Before and After it; a barrier's soak; a gain's scaling, as Healing Received's. StepsText reads them as a line: "-30 as made → Math -30 → -15 (inline) → Answering (queued 1, after) → landed -15". The steps are recorded only where the log keeps entries, or something hears the entity's OnAttributeChangeStep.

Effect Journal

An EffectJournal keeps the life of each effect on the entities it listens to: Listen(gas) one, Follow(list) these and no others, Forget(gas) one, and Dispose() all; what it wrote stays. Its Entries run oldest first, its last Keep of them, 500 unless it's told; Of(effect) and On(entity) filter them, Written counts every one it wrote, and Wrote is raised as each is. It records only while it listens, and an entity no journal listens to pays nothing: the Creator Lab's bottom panel reads one, and the Debugger's Timeline.

Each entry is an EffectJournalEntry: its Kind, its Time and Frame, the Entity it happened on, the Effect, its Spec and its Source, and what its kind tells:

  • Applied: the effect landed, with its first impact's number read as it landed, part by part (Trace, a NumberTrace).
  • Refused: it didn't, and its Refusal says why: the entity is Dead, its Affiliation policy doesn't take the entity, its Requirements don't hold, an Immunity names it, its Chance missed, a Block or Evade Stopped it, or it was asked for again inside its own application (Cycle). Callbacks hear each as OnEffectRefused(spec, refusal).
  • Ticked: its ticks executed, Ticks of them.
  • Impact: a change it made, to its Attribute, by its Change, with its Breakdown through its pipeline.
  • Removed: it came off.
  • Worker: one of its workers heard one of its moments, or one of its dealer's impact workers ran for its hit: the Worker, its WorkerName, the Hook (Application, Tick, Removal or Impact), the Side it ran on (the Target, or the Dealer for a hit, as WORKERS.md says), and the Actions it queued, each Queued, Ran or Dropped.

An entry reads as a line: "Burn landed on Target, from Caster", "Poison refused by Target: immunity", "Burn's Noting heard it tick on Target, queuing Ticked".

Local Data

Local Data is a list of named values stored on the asset. Each row has a Key (a tag) and a value of a kind: Float, Int, String, Bool, Tag, Attribute, Effect, Ability, Entity, Object, MonoBehaviour or ScriptableObject, or a kind of your own, a [Serializable] class made from LocalDataValue<T>, as a point or a colour, which the row's kinds offer beside PlayForge's. A row saves its own value alone. Object fields take project assets, not scene objects. Code reads a value by its type, data.TryGet(out float range) or data.TryGet(out MyPoint point), and a number, a Float or an Int, which can scale, with data.Value.TryReadNumber(context, out float number).

When a GameplayAbilitySystem component initializes, it loads the entity's Local Data into a store of its own. Code and workers read and write that store through the GAS, with TryGetLocalData and SetLocalData. Threshold analysis workers, for example, note there that they have fired. A RuntimeGameplayAbilitySystem loads its identity's rows the same way, the entity asset's where it's made from one.

Used By

The PlayForge assets that refer to this entity, each with its type icon, such as the abilities and items linked to it. Click a row to select the asset. The list follows the project as it changes.
