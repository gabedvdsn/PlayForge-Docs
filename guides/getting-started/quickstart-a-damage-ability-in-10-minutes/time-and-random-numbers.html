Time and random numbers

This section covers where PlayForge's time and random numbers come from, and the clocks an entity or a sequence runs by.

PlayForge reads time through ForgeTime, and draws random numbers through ForgeRandom. They read Unity's Time and Random unless you put in your own:

Call What you put in
ForgeTime.Use(clock) A clock of your own, an IForgeClock.
ForgeRandom.Use(new SeededRandom(seed)) Seeded rolls, which repeat from their seed whatever else draws from Unity's Random.

Each Use returns what it replaced, so you can put it back. Each entity numbers its effects' applications (GameplayEffectSpec.ApplicationId) and its abilities' activations.

An entity's clock

Each entity also has a clock of its own, gas.GetClock(): the game's time, scaled by the entity's TimeScale.

  • The entity's effects run by it, its cooldowns among them.
  • Its abilities' waits and sequences run by it too.

So TimeScale = 0.5f slows one entity and no other. A scale of 0 holds the entity's time still, but it isn't a pause.

A sequence's clock

A sequence can run by a clock of its own:

  • WithClock(UnscaledClock.Instance) runs a pause menu's sequence while the game's time stands still.
  • A ScaledClock runs a sequence at a pace of its own.
  • A packet's Clock, an entity's among them, comes before either.

An ability's Run Sequence task picks a clock in its inspector, with its Clock. Task Sequences has the order a run takes them in.

Fixed ticks

For a run that comes out the same at any frame rate, set ProcessControl's Fixed Tick Rate, as 60. The game then steps in ticks, each a fixed step of its time, as many a frame as the frame's time holds:

  • Every process steps once a tick, its end of frame with it.
  • Each sequence's wait goes on by the tick.
  • Code of your own that waits for a sequence's next frame awaits ForgeTick.Next(token).

0, the default, steps once a frame. The ticks stand still with the game's time, so a sequence on the unscaled clock waits for Unity's frames instead.
