What a load does

  1. Running abilities are interrupted, and the input buffer empties.
  2. The entity lets go of its effects, items and abilities, and the actions queued this frame are dropped. An ability it holds in the slot the save gives it stays as it is, so another entity's effect that it applied still names it.
  3. The save's abilities, items, effects, levels and commits come back.
  4. The tags compile once, and OnRestored fires.

A load tells nothing as it goes:

  • No effect is told as applied or removed, and no attribute as changed.
  • Nothing of an effect's application runs again: not its workers, its commits on application, or its contained effects.
  • A worn item's effects come back from the save, so wearing it again applies none.

The one tag compile tells what the load changed, as any compile does. Then gas.GetCallbacks().OnRestored fires once. Listen to it to refresh what shows the entity, such as a health bar or a hotbar.

The entity's attribute set has to define the attributes the save names. An attribute the catalog names but the save doesn't list loses its commits, since the entity had none when it was saved.

Load between the entity's steps. A restore called from inside the entity's step or its end of frame, as from one of its callbacks, is refused with a warning, because it would empty the lists the step is going through. Load on the next frame instead.
