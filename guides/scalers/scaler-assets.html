Scaler assets

A Scaler asset (Create → PlayForge → Scaler) holds one scaler that many fields can use. A field of the Shared kind reads its Asset's scaler, so an edit to the asset changes every field that uses it, with nothing to copy or propagate. Open shows the asset in a window of its own. Under the field, the asset's curve shows beside its range, as "6–8, levels 1–5", and how many fields share it. A field adjusts the shared values only through its own number and Combine: Scaler × Number with a number of 1.5 reads the asset's values times 1.5, as in Fire Duration × 1.5.

  • The asset's own inspector shows its name, its kind and how many fields use it, its Name, which renames it, as The Forge and the field menus list it, its Description, its Values, and Used By: each field that shares it, by its asset and field. Click a row to select the asset. In The Forge, its summary says its description, its kind, what it reads and how many fields use it.
  • Its Try section reads it at each level of a range, Levels 1 to 5 to start, as an effect's Levels does: a row of what it gives at each, and under it the parts behind the level picked. It reads against a stand-in Caster and Target and a Caller, each with a row for what the scaler asks of it, found as it reads: an attribute on its side, whether the side Has it, its current and its base; and a key the caller sets, whether it's Set and its value, unset reading the scaler's default. A side's Entity fills its attributes from what that entity's attribute set starts each at, at its starting level, and one the set doesn't give, it lacks. A stand-in holds attributes alone, so a number that reads an effect, an item, a tag, a retention group's part or Local Data reads as on an entity with none of them. What's typed is kept for the session, never in the asset. ScalerTry does the same in code: new ScalerTry { From = 1, To = 5 }.Read(scaler).
  • Any magnitude field can share a Scaler asset. An attribute set's element shares one whose scaler is a cached kind, and its Scaler Assets lists only those. A Scaler asset can't hold a Shared scaler of its own.
  • A field whose asset is missing reads as a field with no scaler, and an element whose asset is missing reads its root value. A chain of shared scalers that comes back to its own asset stops after eight steps, rather than run forever.

The Scaler asset was the Scaler Template, whose link and sync settings nothing read, so those settings are gone. So are the old scaler window's curve editor and import window: a scaler's values are its table, and Import Values compares them before it copies.
