What one change runs through, from the verb to the end of frame. Register and Unregister keep the transport value; only a Commit previews its delta.

  1. pre-change workers;
  2. PHASE-2 dispatch;
  3. post-change workers;
  4. impact relay (ImpactData, frame summaries, source-side TrackImpact, AttributeChanged).

Four verbs make up the API, each with its own dispatch:

Verb Dispatch Used by
RegisterModifier(attr, smv, impacters) register/refresh in modifiers + recompute Continuous entries on apply / stack refresh / replace
UnregisterModifier(attr, smv) remove carrier's entries + recompute removal & replacement (see below)
CommitImpact(attr, smv, impacters) permanent per-group commit OnApply / Periodic entries
ModifyAttribute(attr, smv) CommitImpact; a delta-only carrier's delta commits as constant Adds in its retention group init pulse, EnforceScaling propagation, external systems (TryModifyAttribute)

UnregisterModifier fires callbacks like any change, so regulated attributes refresh. Removal used to be silent, which left stale derived values.

It's no hit, though: its ImpactData.Ends holds. So:

  • its effect's workers and components hear no impact;
  • its dealer's impact workers don't run;
  • no Impact Taken or Dealt is sent.

Registration and unregistration are identity-based on the carrier instance. They are never wrapped in synthetic carriers.

Pre-change phase (commits). Before the pre-change callback and workers run, a commit evaluates its impacters once. They are frozen as constants, so a randomized scaler can't roll differently twice.

The commit then previews the incoming delta: ops in order, no bounds or rounding. That delta is what ChangeValue holds, since effect specs arrive as (0,0) transport.

An Inline worker's edit is what commits:

  • untouched → the frozen impacters commit as authored (op semantics, per-impacter groups);
  • adjusted → the new delta commits as one constant Add in the impacters' shared group (mixed groups fall back to the carrier's retention group).

Either way §2.2 applies: post-constraint delta recorded, then Recompute. Armor therefore acts on incoming damage and the clamp comes after: 20 HP, −30 hit, 50% armor → −15 → 5 HP.

Raw deltas (ModifyAttribute) preview as themselves. Register/Unregister are observe-only here, because modifiers are re-derived every recompute, so a one-off adjustment has nowhere to live.

A commit allocates nothing, adjusted or not. It rents its ChangeValue and its frozen impacters and gives them back when it's done.

It rents one set for each commit in progress, since a worker or a listener can commit inside another. An adjusted delta's Adds are frozen in the same impacters. So:

  • a ChangeValue belongs to its commit. A callback or an Inline worker reads it during the call, or copies its Value, and doesn't keep it;
  • a Deferred worker's actions may keep it. A change handed to one is left to them, and its commit takes a new one for the next;
  • the source-side TrackImpact keeps a total, the last impact and a count, not every impact.

A raw delta allocates nothing on its way through either. Its delta's Adds are frozen in the same impacters, and a SourceAttributeImpact keeps the origin it makes.

GenerateSimple makes a new carrier on every call. So a raw delta made every frame keeps its carrier, made once with IAttributeImpactCarrier.GenerateSourceDerivation(source, attribute).

The changes the system makes on someone's behalf, EnforceScaling's rescale and the lifesteal worker's heal, allocate nothing either:

  • each is carried by a carrier kept for its source and attribute, in a table keyed weakly by source, so a source that's gone isn't held;
  • each deferred one is queued in a ModifyAttributeAction that the system takes back once the queue has run it:
    • so a listener of OnActionQueued or OnActionExecuted reads an action during the call;
    • one the queue invalidates isn't taken back;
  • the queue walks its priority buckets by index, and EnqueueRange reads a list or an array by index, so taking an action off allocates nothing.

Null or undefined attribute targets early-out safely: pure tag-grant effects are legal.
