PlayForge — Items

An Item asset describes something an entity can hold and equip, such as a sword or a ring. An entity is anything that runs a gameplay ability system (GAS), such as the player or an enemy. Giving an item puts it in the entity's inventory. Equipping it applies what it grants: its Granted Effects, its Active Ability, its Granted Tags and its workers. Unequipping the item, or removing it from the inventory, takes them away again.

An entity gets items from its Entity asset's Starting Items, or from code through its ItemSystemComponent, with GiveItem, EquipItem, UnequipItem and RemoveItem. The entity's limits on items are set on its Entity asset.

Tags are names, such as State.Stunned, that an entity carries and that requirements check. Each asset stores a tag made from its Name: Iron Sword gives Item.IronSword. The entity carries this tag while the item is in its inventory, equipped or not, and keeps the item's level under it. Each section below matches the inspector section of the same name. A collapsed section shows one line about what it holds, such as the level range or how many effects the item grants.

The inspector's header shows the item's one line, as The Forge's Summary column does: what each effect it gives changes while it's equipped, by its first impact across its levels, then the ability it grants, as "+20 Armor; Blink".

Identity

  • Name: the item's display name. It also gives the asset tag, so renaming the item changes its tag. Update anything that names the old tag.
  • Description: free text for your UI.
  • Asset Tag: read-only, made from the Name: punctuation is dropped, and each word is capitalised and joined. The tag is stored when you edit the Name, and the inspector header shows it after the item's type. If the stored tag differs from the one the Name gives, it shows in warning text with an Update button that stores the new one.
  • Visibility: a tag your UI can read for filtering, such as Always Visible or Not Visible. The Forge lists it. The runtime doesn't act on it.
  • Textures: icons, each paired with a tag. The texture tagged Primary is the item's icon. Without one, the first texture is used.
  • Max Stack Size: how many of the item one inventory slot holds, a number field read at the item's level, as StackSizeOf(spec) reads it. 1 or less, and each is a slot of its own.

Whether an inventory may hold an item more than once is the entity's choice, through its Allow Duplicate Items setting (see ENTITIES.md).

Stacking

A give fills the item's stack first, up to its Max Stack Size, and takes no new slot. A full stack opens another only with Allow Duplicate Items on; without it, what doesn't fit isn't taken. ItemSystemComponent.GiveItem(item, count) takes what fits and returns how many it took, and GiveItem(item, out index) gives one, into the stack if there's room. RemoveItem(item, count) takes stacks off, from the last slot first, and a slot whose last stack comes off goes; RemoveItem(item) still takes the whole slot. A slot's count is its container's ItemStacks.

A stack applies its effects and its ability once. An Item Stacks scaler reads the count of the item that applied the effect, so a magnitude of 5 with Multiply With Scaler grants +5 a stack. As the count changes, an equipped item's effects read it again. An effect that reads an item's stacks where no item in the project applies it reads 1, and the Problems view says so.

Leveling

An item has its own level on each entity that holds it, kept under the item's asset tag. Its Granted Effects take this level, and its Active Ability is granted at it. A level provider is an asset whose level range another asset follows.

  • Link Mode: Standalone (default) keeps the item's own range. Linked To Provider takes the range from a level provider instead.
  • Starting Level and Max Level: the item's own range, by default 1 and 4. Both are at least 1, and the Starting Level is kept within the range.
  • Level Provider, while linked: the asset the range comes from, an Entity, Ability, Effect, Item or Attribute Set. Right-click the field to pick from one type. A link that would make a loop is refused, with an error in the Console. While no provider is set, a warning says the item uses its own range. While linked, Starting Level and Max Level show the range in use, read-only.
  • Link Ability & Effects links the Granted Effects and the Active Ability to this item, so their per-level values follow its range. Unlink Ability & Effects removes the links that point at this item. Each asks first, and one Undo takes it back.

A given item starts at its Starting Level, unless code gives it at another level. Its level then stays between that level and the Max Level. Code changes it through the entity's GAS, with SetLevel or ModifyLevel and the item's asset tag.

The link serves the inspector: the Leveling section, and the scalers of effects linked to this item, read the provider's range. At runtime, the item's level comes from its own Starting Level and Max Level, even while it is linked.

Tags

  • Context Tags describe the item, such as Weapon. The changes made by effects this item applies carry them as context, and workers can filter by them. They are not applied to the entity.
  • Granted Tags are applied to the entity while the item is equipped.

Effects

Granted Effects are applied to the entity while the item is equipped. Each is applied with the item as its origin, so it takes the item's level. Unequipping removes the effects this item applied. It leaves the same effect alone where something else applied it.

Use Infinite effects for bonuses that last while the item is equipped. An Instant effect commits once, when the item is equipped, and unequipping doesn't undo it. A Durational effect ends when its time runs out, even while the item is still equipped.

A line under each effect says how it takes its levels, and Link makes it take this item's. New, beside an empty field, makes an effect linked to this item.

Abilities

  • Active Ability: an ability the entity learns while the item is equipped. It is granted at the item's level when the item is equipped, and removed when the item is unequipped. If the entity can't take it, because of its Max Abilities or Allow Duplicate Abilities settings, the item is still equipped, without the ability.

To make the ability's levels follow the item's, use Link Ability & Effects in Leveling.

Components

An item's components are features it carries, each a class with hooks into its life, added with the list's +. A component hears the item Given to an entity, Equipped, Unequipped and Removed, and Used, as its holder's use of the item's Active Ability ends having paid what it costs. Its context's Restoring says a moment comes from a load, which puts back what the component's earlier moments did, as the effects it applied. A kind that makes no sense twice is offered only while the item holds none of it.

A component of your own derives from AbstractItemComponent and overrides the hooks it needs, each a no-op otherwise. It's the item's, shared by everything that holds it, so what it keeps for one entity's item it keeps through its context: ctx.GetState<T>(this).

Auras

An Aura component holds an effect on those near its holder, while it's equipped, as a banner that inspires the allies round its bearer: each a radius, whom it takes, its holder too or not, and how often it checks (EFFECTS.md).

Uses

A Uses component counts the uses of the item's Active Ability, each a use that paid what it costs, as a potion's sips or a wand's charges, its Uses read at the item's level. With none left, one of the item goes, at the frame's end: a stack of it, its uses whole again for the next, or the item with its last, and its ability with it. A load gives its uses whole. An item with no Active Ability never counts any, which the Problems view says. An item holds one at most.

Set Bonus

A Set Bonus component gives its holder a Bonus effect while it has enough of a set's pieces equipped, as two pieces of a set giving fire resistance and four a flame aura. Each piece names its Set by a tag and carries the set's bonuses, each a component with the Pieces it needs, a number field read at its holder's level. The holder's equipped pieces count each item once, and a bonus holds while they're as many as it needs, once for the holder however many pieces carry it; it comes from one of the pieces, and moves to another as that one leaves while enough stay. A load puts the bonus back with the other effects, and weighs each set once.

Cues

A Cues component plays what the item shows and sounds on its holder: On Equip as it's equipped, While Equipped for as long as it is, and On Use as it's used, as the ability it lends is. A load that puts an equipped item back begins its While Equipped cues and plays no burst. Each list holds cues as an effect's does (EFFECTS.md). An item holds one at most.

Workers

Workers are reusable behaviours, picked from a list, that react to what happens to an entity. This group is subscribed to the entity when the item is equipped, and removed when it is unequipped. Name labels the group. It holds four lists:

  • Attribute workers run before or after an attribute changes, for example to reduce incoming damage.
  • Impact workers inspect the changes the entity deals.
  • Tag workers react when the entity's tags change.
  • Analysis workers examine the frame at its end, for example to detect death.

Each list's + adds an empty worker to pick a kind for. The section's import button copies a worker group from another asset.

On Hit

On Hit, above the workers, holds the effects the item applies as its holder's hits land, while it's equipped: a dagger's bleed, a hammer's armor break, a barrier on a kill. Each entry has:

  • Attribute: what a hit lowers, as Health. A hit is a loss to it that the holder deals another entity. None counts the attribute playing the project's Health role (ATTRIBUTES.md), where it has one.
  • Effects: what it applies. Each effect's own Chance to Apply rolls as it lands, and its hits set the entry off no more, so a shock it applies doesn't shock again. A hit's proc coefficient scales their chance and duration, and 0 sets nothing off (EFFECTS.md).
  • To: the one hit (Target), or the Holder, as a barrier on a kill does.
  • Only On Kill: only the hit that takes the attribute from above 0 to 0 or below.
  • Impact Types: only hits of one of these types, as attacks, so a bleed's ticks, typed apart, set off nothing. None takes any hit. The Problems view warns of a type no effect in the project deals, which never matches.
  • When: a condition read at the hit, the holder its caster and the one hit its target.

Modifiers

Modifiers, under On Hit, change other effects as they're made, while it's equipped: its holder's own, as a boon that adds a burst to its holder's every Chill, or those landing on it, as a cloak that halves the stuns its wearer takes. Each entry is a rule of its own or names a shared Effect Modifier asset (EFFECTS.md).

Lifesteal

An item gives its holder lifesteal by an effect that raises the holder's Lifesteal, the attribute the project's Lifesteal worker names as its Share, one of its Granted Effects while it's equipped, which the Lifesteal worker heals by (WORKERS.md): a vampiric mask's Infinite effect of +0.15 Lifesteal heals its wearer 15% of each blow it deals. Its Lifesteal list is gone.

Local Data

Local Data is a list of named values stored on the asset. Each row has a Key (a tag) and a value of a kind: Float, Int, String, Bool, Tag, Attribute, Effect, Ability, Entity, Object, MonoBehaviour or ScriptableObject, or a kind of your own, a [Serializable] class made from LocalDataValue<T>, as a point or a colour, which the row's kinds offer beside PlayForge's. A row saves its own value alone. Object fields take project assets, not scene objects. Code reads a value by its type, data.TryGet(out float range) or data.TryGet(out MyPoint point), and a number, a Float or an Int, which can scale, with data.Value.TryReadNumber(context, out float number).

PlayForge doesn't read an item's Local Data itself. Your code and workers can, with item.TryGetLocalData(key, out var data). The first row with that key wins.

Used By

The PlayForge assets that refer to this item, each with its type icon, such as an entity that starts with it or an effect linked to it. Click a row to select the asset. The list follows the project as it changes.
