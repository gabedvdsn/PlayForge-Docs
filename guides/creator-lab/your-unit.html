Your unit

In either mode you can play a unit of your own. Put down in the Lab panel's Your unit section puts one of the entity picked above down on the side picked, where the camera looks, and gives you control of it; Take control takes the unit selected, and a bot taken over stops fighting until you let it go, a new fight passing it over and Reset keeping you on its seat's new bot. Release lets it go, and Follow has the camera follow it. The line under the buttons says whom you control.

The controls are the Demo's: right-click the ground to walk there, a foe to attack it, or an ally to walk to it; Q, W, E and R cast its abilities past its attack, in its entity's order, and 1 to 6 its items' active abilities; S stops it. An ability that needs a target waits for a left-click, while a ring on the ground shows its reach, its Cast Range or its Reach: another ability's key casts that one instead, a right-click order lets it go, and Esc cancels it. The click that picks a target selects nothing. The keys are the Lab Controls' own, as the status says them, and the ring's colors, width and material are its too. They read PlayForge's input, ForgeInput, so a game's own input or a test's stands in.

Its orders go through a driver. The lab's walks a unit in a straight line and casts as the lab's units do. A project whose units move and cast their own way makes a Lab Driver asset (Create → PlayForge → Lab Driver) of its own class, overriding the orders its game gives its own way (MoveTo, Attack, Cast, Stop, each doing as the lab's does unless overridden), and names it on its Lab Rules' Driver.
