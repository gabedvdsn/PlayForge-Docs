In code

CreatorLab.Create(library, rules, panels) makes a lab, as the tests do. Its calls are the panels': AddUnit and AddDummy, ApplyEffect, CastAt, GiveItem, Drop (at a point of the screen) and DropAt (on a unit or a point), Select, SetMode, ResetDummies, Clear, and Step, Paused, Speed and StepFrame for its time. A library is an ILabLibrary: in the editor every asset of the project (EditorLabLibrary), in code a ListLibrary of the assets given; a game's own build gets ListLibrary.Of(project), the assets its Forge Project's catalog names, which the lab plays where no editor gives it a library. A library offers projects to switch between through ILabLibrary.Projects, Project and UseProject, which have defaults. The unit you control is ControlledUnit, given by PutDownOwnUnit, TakeControl and ReleaseControl, its camera by Follow; the lab's controls are its Controls, a LabControls, whose Driver comes before its rules'. A game's own player can be a LabControls too, as the Demo's is: its unit set on it, or its ControlledUnit() overridden.

What it shows of its working is its Showing, a LabShowing: its Journal, an EffectJournal over every unit; its Stops, added with Add(stop), and StoppedBy and StoppedFor as one fires, Resume() to let it go and StepTick(); its Watches, pinned with Pin(watch); and its Log of ForgeLog's messages.

A build can leave the lab out by defining PLAYFORGE_NO_LAB. The Demo uses the lab's units, so it goes too.
