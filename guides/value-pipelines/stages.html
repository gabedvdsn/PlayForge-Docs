Stages

Each stage has a Label, the name a breakdown gives it, and Enabled, which lets the value pass it by.

  • Multiply: the value times a number read for the hit. A bonus, × the caster's Damage Amplification read as 1 + x; a reduction, × the target's Damage Resistance read as what's left; an armor curve; a bonus that reads the target, as 75% more while the target is above 90% of its health.
  • Add: a number added to the value's size. A flat bonus of 10 makes a blow of −100 one of −110, and a heal of 50 one of 60.
  • Chance: on a chance, the value times a number: a critical hit. It rolls once for the hit, and the breakdown notes it. Its Roll makes it pseudo-random or lucky (Effects: Requirements).
  • Clamp: the value's size kept between two numbers, its sign as it was.
  • Absorb: a loss taken first from another attribute of the one hit, as a barrier or a shield takes a blow before health. What it soaks comes off that attribute at the frame's end, and the rest goes on. An attribute with the Barrier role soaks the losses to the one it absorbs for with no stage at all (Attributes).
  • Block and Evade: a Chance, read as the effect lands, that stops it. Each rolls once, before anything of the effect lands, and where it comes up nothing of it does, its damage, tags, contained effects and on-hits, and its ticks aren't rolled again; the one hit hears Blocked or Evaded, and the dealer Hit Blocked or Hit Evaded. A defender brings one through an incoming modifier's Change Impact, as Tougher Times blocks the hits landing on its holder by a stacking curve of its stacks (Effects: Modifiers). Its Stops says what it can stop: Losses, the default, an effect that lowers its attribute, an add below 0, a multiplier below 1 or an override below where it stands, so a heal lands past it unrolled; or Any Impact, heals and buffs too. As the hit commits, it passes the value on.
  • Execute: a hit that would leave the one hit with less than its Share of its Attribute's base, the Health role's where it names none, takes the rest, as Culling Blade; the breakdown notes it.

A project writes its own stage by deriving from AbstractValueStage and overriding Apply(value, in run). run.Context reads the hit's caster, target and level, as any number does, and run.Note notes what the stage did.
