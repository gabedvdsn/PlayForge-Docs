Value Pipelines

A Value Pipeline is an asset that takes a value as it lands, sends it through ordered stages, and gives what comes out. An effect's impact names one as its calculation, as an Unreal effect names its execution calculation: each hit of that impact goes through the pipeline's stages, so a project writes once how a hit's damage is worked out, and a new item that changes every hit is a stage, not an edit to each attack.

It's generic: the value is any number an impact lands with, a blow, a heal, a drain. Create one from Create > PlayForge > Value Pipeline, or with New beside an impact's Pipeline field, which makes one named for the effect and uses it; once the field names one, Open shows it. A Crowd Control Rules or Effect Modifier field has the same two.

Not a Scaler asset

A Scaler asset is a number worked out from its context: a level table, × an attribute, a condition. A number field reads it wherever a number is read.

A Value Pipeline transforms a value it's given. It runs once for each hit, as the hit lands, with the caster and the target both known, and its stages can do what a number can't: roll a chance once for the hit and keep it, route part of the value into a barrier, and say what each stage did.

A stage's own numbers are number fields, so a stage reads × Attribute, conditions, curves and Scaler assets as any field does.

When it runs

It runs for what an impact commits: an instant impact's hit, and each tick of a periodic one. A lasting impact, as +10 Strength while a buff holds, is read again whenever its attribute changes, so a pipeline doesn't run it.

A modifier's Change Impact brings a stage of its own (EFFECTS.md): a hit runs it after its pipeline, or alone where it has none, and the breakdown keeps it with the pipeline's steps; a lasting impact takes it on its magnitude.

Stages

Each stage has a Label, the name a breakdown gives it, and Enabled, which lets the value pass it by.

  • Multiply: the value times a number read for the hit. A bonus, × the caster's Damage Amplification read as 1 + x; a reduction, × the target's Damage Resistance read as what's left; an armor curve; a bonus that reads the target, as 75% more while the target is above 90% of its health.
  • Add: a number added to the value's size. A flat bonus of 10 makes a blow of −100 one of −110, and a heal of 50 one of 60.
  • Chance: on a chance, the value times a number: a critical hit. It rolls once for the hit, and the breakdown notes it. Its Roll makes it pseudo-random or lucky (EFFECTS.md).
  • Clamp: the value's size kept between two numbers, its sign as it was.
  • Absorb: a loss taken first from another attribute of the one hit, as a barrier or a shield takes a blow before health. What it soaks comes off that attribute at the frame's end, and the rest goes on. An attribute with the Barrier role soaks the losses to the one it absorbs for with no stage at all (ATTRIBUTES.md).
  • Block and Evade: a Chance, read as the effect lands, that stops it. Each rolls once, before anything of the effect lands, and where it comes up nothing of it does, its damage, tags, contained effects and on-hits, and its ticks aren't rolled again; the one hit hears Blocked or Evaded, and the dealer Hit Blocked or Hit Evaded. A defender brings one through an incoming modifier's Change Impact, as Tougher Times blocks the hits landing on its holder by a stacking curve of its stacks (EFFECTS.md). Its Stops says what it can stop: Losses, the default, an effect that lowers its attribute, an add below 0, a multiplier below 1 or an override below where it stands, so a heal lands past it unrolled; or Any Impact, heals and buffs too. As the hit commits, it passes the value on.
  • Execute: a hit that would leave the one hit with less than its Share of its Attribute's base, the Health role's where it names none, takes the rest, as Culling Blade; the breakdown notes it.

A project writes its own stage by deriving from AbstractValueStage and overriding Apply(value, in run). run.Context reads the hit's caster, target and level, as any number does, and run.Note notes what the stage did.

A typical damage pipeline

  1. Amplification: Multiply by the caster's Damage Amplification (a Bonus Share, read as 1 + x).
  2. Critical: Chance, the caster's Crit Chance, × 2.
  3. Armor: Multiply by a curve of the target's Armor.
  4. Resistance: Multiply by the target's Damage Resistance (a Resistance, read as 1 − x).
  5. Barrier: Absorb into the target's Barrier.

Every damage effect names it, so a blow of 100 from a caster with 50% amplification, on a target with 20% resistance, lands as 120.

The Demo's blows go through two such pipelines, Physical Damage and Magic Damage in its Effects/Pipelines: each multiplies a blow by its dealer's Attack or Magic Amplification, a Stacking Curve of it read as 1 + it, then by what its target's Armor leaves, 100 ÷ (100 + Armor), or its Magic Resistance, 1 − it, kept between 0 and 1. Its Physical and Magic Strike templates name them, and so does each blow that takes one, so a new attack takes its kind's from its template.

The breakdown

An effect's spec keeps its last run as a Breakdown: the value in, each stage's before and after with its note, and the value out. spec.Breakdown.ToString() says it in a line: -100 → Amplification -150 → Resistance -120. A combat log, a debugger or a test reads it.

Trying a pipeline

A Value Pipeline's inspector has a Try section under its stages, which puts a value through them at each level of a range, as a Scaler asset's Try reads a scaler:

  • Value In is the value a hit lands with, going in: a loss below 0, as a blow of 100 is −100, and a gain above.
  • Chances Come Up settles each roll, so a try gives the same each time: on, each chance a stage rolls comes up where it can, a critical strike doubling the hit and a block stopping it; off, each misses where it can. A chance of 1 always comes up and one of 0 never does.
  • Levels, 1 to 5 to start, are the hit's levels, for a stage whose number scales by level.
  • Its Caster, Target and Caller are a Scaler asset's Try's: a row for each attribute a stage reads on a side, as a Multiply stage's Damage Amplification or an Absorb stage's Barrier, typed or filled from an Entity, and a row for each key a caller sets.

Its Out row says what lands at each level. Click one to see its steps under the table: the value in, each stage's value in and out with its note, as "critical", and what comes out. A Block or Evade stage rolls first, as it does as an effect lands, and one that comes up stops the hit: nothing of it lands, and its level shows "—". No effect is behind a try, so what an Absorb stage soaks comes off no one, and a stage of your own that reads run.Carrier reads none. What's typed is kept for the session, never in the asset.

ValuePipelineTry does the same in code, new ValuePipelineTry { Value = -100f }.Read(pipeline), and pipeline.Run(value, in context) puts a value through a pipeline against a number context of your own, with no effect behind it.
