PlayForge — Workers

Workers are the small rules that react to what happens to an entity: a floor that keeps Health above 1, a share of each blow sent back, a rage at half Health, a burst as a tag comes off. This page maps each kind: when it runs, on whose system and for whom, and what its context holds. RECIPES.md builds common rules from them.

Where workers come from

  • An attribute set's Worker Group: the entity built from the set holds its workers as it lives.
  • An entity's Worker Group (its Entity asset): the same, as the entity's own.
  • Its project's Workers: every entity in a Forge Project holds the workers its settings list, under Workers in Project Settings → PlayForge, after its own, as it registers. A project starts with the Lifesteal worker, whose Share is yours to name.
  • An item's, an ability's or an effect's Worker Group: the entity holds them while the item is equipped, the ability known, or the effect lasts on it. Two of an effect lasting give its group once.
  • An effect's Workers: effect workers, which run for that effect's own events.
  • Built in: an On Hit entry gives its holder an impact worker.

Lifesteal

The Lifesteal worker, an impact worker, heals its holder a share of each workable loss it deals another: the value of its holder's Share, the attribute it names, as 0.15 for 15%; with none it heals nothing. The losses that count are to its Receives attribute, and it heals its Heals attribute, each the Health role's where it names none, so a mana burn can heal Health. An effect raises the share while it should, as an effect has no lifesteal of its own. Only hits of the Impact Types it names count, as attacks; with none, any; so a second worker on another attribute, of spells' types, makes a spell vamp. A worker that runs once for a hit, as this one, runs once however many of it its holder has, its project's and its own: the first of the kind to take the hit. It allocates nothing, healing or not.

Inline or deferred. An attribute worker's Execution says how it acts. An Inline one runs inside the change, can edit the change in Pre Change, and must not change attributes itself, which would run workers inside workers. A Deferred one returns actions instead, as impact, analysis and effect workers do; the entity's action queue runs them at its end of frame, where they can change anything. A focused attribute worker, one that watches a single attribute, sets its Timing (Pre Change, Post Change or both), its Sign Policy (the changes it takes: losses, gains or either), and Allow Self Modification (whether it takes changes whose source and target are the same entity).

The action queue

What a deferred worker returns, and whatever else is queued to an entity (GetActionQueue().Enqueue), waits in the entity's action queue for its end of frame.

  • When it runs. Four passes in each end of frame: the actions queued since the last; then those the tag workers' activations and resolutions queue; then those their ticks queue; then those the analysis workers, the Health watch and the effects' conditions queue. An action queued as a pass runs joins that pass.

  • In what order. By priority, higher first (ActionPriority, from Critical's 100 down to Cleanup's −300), and in the order queued within one. Enqueue(action, priorityOverride) puts one at another priority.

  • What's heard. Each action is heard as it's queued (OnActionQueued), then once more as it's done with:

    • run (OnActionExecuted);
    • dropped unrun (OnActionInvalidated): its IsValid said no as its turn came, it was taken off, or its entity had ended;
    • failed (OnActionFailed, with what it threw): its IsValid or its Execute threw. It's written to the Console as well, and the rest of the queue runs on.

    The queue's events and the entity's callbacks both tell them. The frame's summary keeps the dropped and the failed, and an EffectJournal settles a worker's action as Ran, Dropped or Failed.

  • Taken off. Remove(action) takes one off, RemoveWhere(match) every one it picks, and Clear() all of them, each heard as dropped; the rest keep their order.

  • An entity that ends. Its queue closes (IsClosed): what waited is dropped and heard, and an action queued to it after is dropped at once, so nothing waits on it for good.

  • Another entity's queue. An action queued to another entity runs at that entity's end of frame: this frame if its end of frame is still to come, by the order the entities step in, or else the next.

  • The guard. A pass runs 10,000 actions at most, so an action that queues another for good can't hold the frame. Those left past it wait for the next pass, and the queue writes an error.

Hearing workers run

An entity tells its workers' runs to whoever listens to its callbacks, and to no one else, so a worker no one hears costs nothing more:

  • OnEffectWorkerRan: one of its effects' workers heard one of the effect's moments, its Hook (Application, Tick, Removal or Impact), on its Side (the Target, or the Dealer for a hit, as the map says), with the System it ran on and the actions it Queued. A tick, which its workers hear every frame, is told only as one executes or a worker queues something.
  • OnImpactWorkerRan: one of its impact workers ran for a hit it dealt, with its Execution and what it queued.
  • OnAttributeChangeStep: a step a change to one of its attributes took, an attribute worker's, with its Timing, its Execution, the change Before and After and what it Queued; a barrier's soak; or a gain's scaling. The combat log keeps each hit's steps (ENTITIES.md).

An EffectJournal hears all of it for the entities it's given (ENTITIES.md).

Workable and unworkable

A change is workable unless it says it isn't (SourcedModifiedAttributeValue.ImpactIsWorkable). An unworkable one is a change the system makes on its own account (GenerateUnworkable), as the pulse each attribute starts with, or a transfer's two sides. Only a workable change:

  • reaches impact workers, unless a worker's Accept Unworkable Impact is on;
  • is soaked by a barrier as a loss, and scaled by its attribute's Gains Scaled By as a gain, as Healing Received;
  • sends the core's Impact Dealt and Impact Taken events, and counts toward the kill: the killer is whoever dealt the last workable loss to the Health role's attribute;
  • counts for lifesteal, and is returned by Return Damage;
  • is written in the combat log as a hit, taken or dealt; another is a Change.

Attribute workers run for every change, workable or not; one that cares reads ctx.Change.Value.ImpactIsWorkable.

Traps

  • An impact worker runs for the dealer. Its context's System is the dealer, and the one hit is ImpactData.Target. A worker that took System for the one hit applied its holder's effects to the target, and read the target's items for its holder's.
  • An effect worker's Impact hook runs on the dealer's side too. Its System is the effect's caster, where its Application, Tick and Removal hooks' System is the one the effect is on.
  • A stacking effect's periodic tick lands once for each stack, while its lasting change counts once. A number that reads the effect's stacks on each tick multiplies again: Hangover, which read its stacks, took 100 a tick where 20 was meant. Read the stacks in a lasting change, or leave them out of a periodic one. The Problems view warns of it.
  • What a worker sets in motion waits for the frame's end. Deferred actions, deaths and the kills they give, and an aura's effect coming off a dead holder all land at the entity's end of frame, so one area's hits resolve together (EFFECTS.md).
